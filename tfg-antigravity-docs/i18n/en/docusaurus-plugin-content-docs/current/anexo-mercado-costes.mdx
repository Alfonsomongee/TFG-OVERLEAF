  <AnnexKeyQuestion>
    Why did the price signals from the electricity market and balancing services fail to reflect the growing dynamic vulnerability of the peninsular system?
  </AnnexKeyQuestion>

  <AnnexThesisBox>
    On April 28, the marginalist market settled energy at normal or even low price levels due to the peak in renewable generation at zero marginal cost. However, this commercial cheapening concealed a dynamic security cost that the current market does not explicitly remunerate: inertia and short-circuit power. The price signals from the day-ahead market and the pan-European balancing services (PICASSO) proved blind to the physical fragility of the system, demonstrating that the current market design lacks mechanisms to value resilience in real time.
  </AnnexThesisBox>

  <AnnexBlackoutRelevance>
    This annex compiles OMIE price records, deviation costs, and balancing service billing that evidence the disconnect between market economics and the physical security of the grid on April 28.
  </AnnexBlackoutRelevance>

  <AnnexMethodNote type="reconstruccion">
    The economic documentation is presented thematically to assess market behavior and price signals resulting from the supply interruption.
