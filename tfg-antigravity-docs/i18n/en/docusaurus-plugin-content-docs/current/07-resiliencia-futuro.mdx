# Resilience and Future of Electrical Systems (Chapter 7)

The structural lesson of April 28, 2025 resides not in the collapse itself, but in the class of vulnerability it revealed. Forensic analysis demonstrated that the voltage zero was neither an active power reserve failure nor an operational error: it was the terminal manifestation of fundamental incompatibility between the physics of a system dominated by <GlossaryLink term="IBR">inverters</GlossaryLink> and a technical-regulatory framework designed for synchronous networks.

The structural scenario enabling April 28---high penetration of <GlossaryLink term="IBR">Inverter-Based Resources (IBR)</GlossaryLink>, scarcity of coupled synchronous generation, and depressed net demand---was not a climate anomaly, but the forewarning of conditions that will reproduce with increasing frequency as the generation fleet decarbonizes.

---

## The Physics of Systemic Fragility

### Inertia Degradation

Dynamic frequency stability is governed by the *Swing Equation*:

$$\frac{2H}{f_0} \, \frac{df}{dt} = P_m - P_e - D \cdot \Delta f$$

where $H$ is the equivalent <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink> constant (seconds), $f_0$ nominal frequency (50 Hz), $df/dt$ the <GlossaryLink term="RoCoF">RoCoF</GlossaryLink> (Rate of Change of Frequency), $P_m$ mechanical power in synchronous rotors, $P_e$ net demanded power.

Reduction of $H$ implies that any power imbalance translates into proportionally sharper <GlossaryLink term="RoCoF">RoCoF</GlossaryLink>, shortening the temporal window for regulation. Wind and solar inverters operating in conventional mode—<GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">*grid-following* (GFL)</GlossaryLink>—are decoupled from frequency through DC links and **provide no inherent electromechanical <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink>**.

At the moment of voltage zero, the System Operator certified available <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink> of $H = 2{,}3$ s, exceeding the ENTSO-E threshold of 2.0 s. The incident **was not** a frequency collapse due to synchronous mass deficit: <GlossaryLink term="RoCoF">RoCoF</GlossaryLink> did not exceed 1 Hz/s until an advanced cascade phase. This nuance is determinant: <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink> deficit was not the cause of April 28, but will reduce safety margins as decarbonization advances.

### Short Circuit Power Collapse

Degradation of <GlossaryLink term="Potencia de cortocircuito">Short Circuit Power ($S_{sc}$)</GlossaryLink> is the dimension most directly linked to April 28. Conventional synchronous generators can inject fault currents of 5–7 times their nominal value. Inverters limit injection to 1.1–1.2 p.u. to prevent semiconductor fusion.

Parametrization is the ***Short Circuit Ratio*** (SCR):

$$\mathrm{SCR} = \frac{S_{sc,\mathrm{PCC}}}{P_{\mathrm{IBR}}}$$

| Category | SCR Threshold | Operational Implication |
|----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Strong Grid** | SCR > 3 | <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">GFL</GlossaryLink> inverters stable. Distance protections selective. |
| **Weak Grid** | 2 ≤ SCR ≤ 3 | Degradation of <GlossaryLink term="Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)">PLL</GlossaryLink> stability margin. Adverse interaction risk. |
| **Very Weak Grid** | SCR < 2 | <GlossaryLink term="Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)">PLLs</GlossaryLink> prone to loss of <GlossaryLink term="Sincronismo">synchronism</GlossaryLink>. Protections lose directionality. |

Wide zones of the Iberian Peninsula operated as "very weak grids" with SCR < 2 prior to April 28. The cause was structural: massive solar production displaced combined cycles, whose disconnection eliminated precisely the <GlossaryLink term="Potencia de cortocircuito">$S_{sc}$</GlossaryLink> sources providing nodal stiffness.

### Geometric Paradox: $S_{\max}$ Constraint and P-Q Conflict

Maximum apparent capacity of an inverter is bounded by:

$$S_{\max} = \sqrt{P^2 + Q^2}$$

When a photovoltaic plant experiences cloud clearing, its <GlossaryLink term="MPPT">MPPT</GlossaryLink> algorithm commands ramps of active power of thousands of MW/h. To not exceed thermal limit, the controller must simultaneously reduce its margin of <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">reactive power</GlossaryLink>.

The paradox resides in that this is exactly the instant when the sharp increase in $P$ over a grid with $\mathrm{SCR} < 2$ causes severe overvoltage in local nodes, requiring massive reactive absorption. The inverter faces **two physically incompatible obligations**: maximize $P$ for market and maximize $Q$ for voltage stability.

This intrinsic contradiction of <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">*grid-following*</GlossaryLink> mode explains why April 28 was not an energy collapse, but a control collapse: **the system had sufficient resources but lacked the control architecture necessary to mobilize them coherently**.

## Enabling Technologies

### BESS with *Grid-Forming* Control

A ***<GlossaryLink term="GFM (Grid-Forming)">grid-forming</GlossaryLink>*** (GFM) inverter operates as a Thévenin equivalent: autonomously synthesizes a voltage reference ($V\angle\delta$) behind a virtual impedance. By imposing this voltage vector instantaneously, it injects or absorbs active and reactive current inherently against grid variations, **without depending on external phase measurements**.

When backed by a battery bank (***BESS-GFM***), it provides:

1. **Synthetic Inertia**: continuously measures $df/dt$ and adjusts output power proportionally, releasing or absorbing energy in tens of milliseconds, emulating rotating mass.

2. **Fast Frequency Response (<GlossaryLink term="FFR">FFR</GlossaryLink>)**: upon <GlossaryLink term="RoCoF">RoCoF</GlossaryLink> threshold exceedance, injects predefined active power subcyclically, slowing rate of decline.

Verified operational results show that 450 MW integration under subcyclic <GlossaryLink term="FFR">FFR</GlossaryLink> scheme reduced critical <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink> threshold from 100,000 MW·s to 88,000 MW·s—12% decline reflecting functional equivalence between sub-second power electronics and synchronous machine kinetic mass.

### Synchronous Compensators

***Synchronous compensators*** (SynCons) are rotative machines operated at no load that freely exchange <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">reactive power</GlossaryLink>. Their contribution is twofold:

1. **Short Circuit Power**: can inject fault currents of 300–400% nominal during first cycles, increasing SCR and providing electrical stiffness for correct protection operation.

2. **Genuine Rotational Inertia**: kinetic energy available in first millisecond of transient, without algorithm mediation, immediately slowing <GlossaryLink term="RoCoF">RoCoF</GlossaryLink>.

### Hybrid Architecture

**No single technology**, deployed in isolation, is sufficient:

- **BESS-GFM**: response speed and <GlossaryLink term="Black Start">*Black Start*</GlossaryLink> capability, but limited in short-circuit current.
- **SynCons**: genuine <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">rotational inertia</GlossaryLink> and high <GlossaryLink term="Potencia de cortocircuito">short-circuit power</GlossaryLink>, but without subcyclic agility or active energy management.

Resilience requires combining distributed BESS-GFM deployment at network nodes with strategic SynCon installation in enclaves most exposed to very weak grid conditions.

## Regulatory Framework: From P.O. 7.4 to Mandatory *Grid-Forming*

### Pre-existing Deficiencies

P.O. 7.4 in force at blackout time had not been substantially revised in twenty-five years. It presented two critical deficiencies:

1. **Participation Asymmetry**: bulk of renewable fleet (82% at collapse) operated at fixed power factor, unable to dynamically inject or absorb reactive power.

2. **Dead Band**: conventional generators exempted from acting if 400 kV voltage remained between 405–410 kV, step-wise response structurally incompatible with capacitive transient speed.

### P.O. 7.4 Revision and VOLTAIRE System

CNMC Resolution of June 12, 2025 (BOE-A-2025-13076) reformulated P.O. 7.4 under **dynamic remunerated service delivery** model: precise real-time *setpoint* tracking telemetrically sent by Electrical Control Center (CECOEL) through Renewable Energy Control Center (CECRE).

The **VOLTAIRE** system operates in two hierarchical layers:

- **Tertiary Regulation**: globally optimizes voltage profile through optimal reactive load flows.
- **Secondary Regulation**: sends real-time directives with 4 s resolution, closing control loop at park scale.

New remuneration scheme introduces:
- **€2/MVArh** for real-time service during hours with positive net production.
- For nighttime hours (<GlossaryLink term="Efecto Ferranti">Ferranti effect</GlossaryLink>), remuneration indexed to Daily Average Price.

### European NC RfG 2.0

**NC RfG 2.0** applies with technology-agnostic approach:

| Type | Power | GFM Requirement | Timeline |
|------|---------|---------------:|----------|
| **Type A** | < 1 MW | Voluntary | N/A |
| **Type B** | 1–50 MW | Mandatory | Max 3 years |
| **Type C** | > 50 MW | Mandatory exhaustive | 3 years post-adoption |
| **Type D** | ≥ 110 kV | Type C + testing | 3 years post-adoption |
| **ESM (BESS)** | Variable | Per category | Per category |

## Essential Reliability Services Markets

### The *Headroom* Problem

For a <GlossaryLink term="GFM (Grid-Forming)">GFM</GlossaryLink> inverter to act as voltage source under severe <GlossaryLink term="RoCoF">RoCoF</GlossaryLink>, it must obligatorily maintain reserve of apparent capacity ($S_{\max}$) unused in steady state. This reserve—termed ***headroom***—requires either deliberately limiting active power in daily market or oversizing <GlossaryLink term="IGBT">IGBT</GlossaryLink> power stage drastically increasing CAPEX. Without direct economic compensation, neither option is commercially viable.

### Market Mechanisms: Three Vectors

**1. Regional Markets for Synthetic Inertia and Fast Frequency Containment**

Auctions where payment for capacity is awarded to assets capable of providing inertial support in margins below 5 milliseconds. BESS systems receive simultaneous revenue from energy arbitrage, frequency regulation, and synthetic inertia and <GlossaryLink term="FFR">FFR</GlossaryLink> payments.

**2. Geographic Signals for Short-Circuit Level**

Unlike frequency (global metric), <GlossaryLink term="SCR (Short Circuit Ratio)">SCR</GlossaryLink> and overvoltages are radically local phenomena. Markets adopt granular spatial price signals: discounted connection tariffs in nodes with $\mathrm{SCR} < 2$.

**3. Ex-ante Remuneration for Pure Stability Investments**

For infrastructure like SynCons, regulation contemplates service-cost-based remuneration models (*Rate of Return Regulation*) or long-term awards protecting capital investment.

### Reference Models

Systems from EirGrid (Ireland) and ERCOT (Texas) constitute verified operational references: both faced earlier challenges of penetrations exceeding 75%. EirGrid's DS3 program fragmented stability provision into 14 specialized products including Synchronous Inertial Response (SIR), <GlossaryLink term="FFR">Fast Frequency Response (FFR)</GlossaryLink>, and Steady-State Reactive Power (SSRP).

## Conclusion: From Free Externalities to Paid Services

The lesson that April 28, 2025 inscribes in European regulatory design has structural scope: **the physical attributes that synchronous systems provided inherently—<GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink>, <GlossaryLink term="Potencia de cortocircuito">short-circuit power</GlossaryLink>, autonomous voltage control—have ceased to be free externalities and become services whose provision must be explicitly defined, verified, and remunerated**. Pure marginal energy model is structurally incapable of remunerating these attributes. Without that economic translation of resilience, no technical requirement of <GlossaryLink term="NC RfG">NC RfG 2.0</GlossaryLink> will prove sustainable in the medium term.
