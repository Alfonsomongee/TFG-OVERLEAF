On April 28, 2025, the Iberian synchronous system suffered a voltage collapse that destroyed more than 15 GW of generation in a matter of seconds (leading to the interruption of approximately 25.2 GW of demand) and left approximately 57 million people without electricity supply. In terms of magnitude and propagation speed, the event is unprecedented in the continental European system in over twenty years. This work is not a chronological reconstruction of the event: it is a critical and comparative analysis of the technical, regulatory, and operational narratives that have competed to explain it.

## Purpose and Scope

The collapse —characterized by a cascade of disconnections associated with overvoltage phenomena in a context of high penetration of inverter-based resources (IBR)—
has generated profound interpretive disagreement regarding its root causes and the
allocation of responsibilities. The investigation examines three predominant
institutional perspectives:

*(Note: the analysis operates on four primary reports —Government, REE, IIT-ICAI/AELEC, and ENTSO-E— grouped into three perspectives, given that the Government and REE reports share fundamental causal premises.)*

Administration Perspective (Government / REE, the System Operator, subsidiary of the holding company Redeia). The report from the Analysis Committee of the National Security Council interprets the collapse from the standpoint of regulatory compliance. Its thesis attributes the worsening of the event to a deficit in inductive reactive power absorption that prevented containing the voltage escalation, in contravention of the requirements of Operating Procedure P.O. 7.4.

Generator Sector Perspective (ICAI / AELEC Report). The expert analysis by IIT-ICAI and Compass Lexecon shifts responsibility towards specific operational decisions made by REE. The central thesis questions the meshing maneuver executed by the operator, arguing that it increased the capacitive contribution of the network in a scenario of low synchronous strength and generated an overvoltage pulse amplified by observability limitations on the 220 kV network —a phenomenon termed Tap-Lag.

European Operator Perspective (ENTSO-E). ENTSO-E's factual report adopts a continental synchronous area perspective: it analyzes the propagation of inter-area oscillations and questions the sufficiency of the N−1 Criterion against
ultra-fast dynamic phenomena characteristic of power-electronics-dominated
systems.
The scope of the work is structured around contrasting these three narratives with European _Network Codes_ —particularly the RfG Regulation— and with modern principles of voltage stability in inverter-dominated systems, aiming to identify structural lessons, methodological limitations, and regulatory gaps that condition the resilience of the European electricity system in the transition towards decarbonization. The narrative analysis is based on an electromagnetic reconstruction of the transient, built upon PMU and WAMS records, and is compatible with the oscillographic evidence published by the four agents.


## The Structural Vulnerability of the Iberian System

The energy transition has profoundly altered the dynamics of the peninsular system. The accelerated replacement of synchronous plants by IBR reduces the rotating masses coupled to the grid and, with them, two critical parameters simultaneously: system inertia (*H*) —which
fell to zonal values between 1.3 s and 1.8 s in the southern peninsula, below the 2 s threshold that technical literature associates with compromised dynamic
margins— and the available short-circuit power at the nodes. This
dual reduction does not only affect the frequency–active power axis: it critically erodes the system's ability to dynamically absorb reactive power and sustain voltage during fast transients, which is precisely the axis around which the collapse unfolded.

This metamorphosis was neither gradual nor imperceptible: it had concrete operational consequences. Available short-circuit power fell in parallel with the loss of synchronous mass —inverters only contribute 1.1–1.2 times their nominal power compared to the 5–7 times of synchronous generators— and the dynamic margin for reactive absorption became limited precisely during periods of highest solar penetration, such as midday on April 28, 2025.

On the morning of the incident, the system operated with high penetration of non-synchronous renewable generation (whose detailed structure is described in the Technical Context section) under valley demand conditions. To balance generation, REE disconnected practically all available thermal units, bringing the number of synchronous generators to historic lows. The IBR fleet operated almost entirely in grid-following mode:
inverters that require stable external voltage and frequency as a synchronization reference. As synchronous units disappeared, the system lost its primary mechanism for dynamic reactive power absorption and, with it, the natural damping against voltage transients.

Added to this internal fragility is the structural isolation of the Iberian Peninsula. With a cross-border interconnection capacity of 7.9% of peak demand —well below community targets— the Iberian system cannot import dynamic stability from the continental core in emergency situations. The confluence of low synchronous strength and relative network isolation configured the vulnerability that made it impossible to halt the overvoltage cascade at 12:33:29.741 CEST.

:::note Geographic Scope
The collapse simultaneously affected Spain and Portugal. REN's (Redes Energéticas Nacionais) system was electrically synchronized with REE via high-voltage interconnections; when the voltage zero occurred in the Spanish peninsular system, the Portuguese network collapsed in milliseconds. Demand without supply in Portugal reached approximately 5.8 GW, and full restoration of the Portuguese system required approximately 12 hours (compared to approximately 15.5 hours for the Spanish system). Chapters 4 and 7c analyze the Portuguese dimension in detail. Throughout this work, "Iberian system" designates the joint synchronous area ES+PT, and "peninsular system" is used exclusively when the analysis is restricted to the Spanish network.
:::

## Methodology: Forensic Triangulation and Cross-Validation

The investigation adopts a comparative forensic analysis approach, structured in three phases:

1. Compilation of primary sources. Systematic study of official expert reports, supported by the processing of over 170 GB of technical records from the System Operator, including SCADA telemetry and oscillographic records.

2. Triangulation of narratives. Systematic contrast of the Administration's thesis against the generator sector's expert analysis around the two main axes of disagreement: the management of the meshing maneuver and the unobservability of the 220 kV network.

3. Cross-technical validation. Cross-referencing of chronological sequences with NREL's independent analysis of inter-area oscillations, obtained from continental-coverage Phasor Measurement Unit (PMU) records —data whose institutional independence allows
arbitration between divergent versions.


Large language models have been used as a documentary assistance tool —classification, synthesis, and extraction of critical data— given the exceptional volume of technical documentation generated after the incident. Their application has been strictly limited to documentary organization: they have known limitations when faced with fast dynamic phenomena such as the Tap-Lag mechanism or the UFLS paradox. The
technical comparison, causality assessment, and conclusions of the work
are the sole responsibility of the author's judgment.
