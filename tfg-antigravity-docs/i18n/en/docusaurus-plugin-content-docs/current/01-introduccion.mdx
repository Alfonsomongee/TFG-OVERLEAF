<GlitchTitle>Introduction</GlitchTitle>

On 28 April 2025, the Iberian synchronous system suffered a voltage collapse that destroyed more than **15 GW of generation within seconds** and left nearly **60 million people** without electricity. In terms of magnitude and propagation speed, the event has no precedent in the continental European power system in recent decades. This work is not a chronological reconstruction of the blackout: it is a critical, comparative analysis of the competing technical, regulatory and operational narratives formulated in its aftermath.

## Scope and objectives

The collapse — characterised by a cascade of disconnections triggered by overvoltage phenomena in a context of high penetration of <GlossaryLink term="IBR">Inverter-Based Resources (_IBR_</GlossaryLink>) — has generated deep interpretive disagreement over its root causes and the allocation of technical and operational responsibility. The research examines three dominant institutional narratives:

**Government / <GlossaryLink term="REE">Redeia</GlossaryLink> narrative.** The report of the National Security Council's Analysis Committee interprets the collapse through the lens of regulatory compliance. Its central thesis attributes the escalation to a deficit in inductive <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">reactive power</GlossaryLink> absorption that prevented the containment of the voltage surge, in contravention of the requirements established in Operating Procedure P.O. 7.4.

**Generator sector narrative (ICAI / AELEC Report).** The independent expert analysis by IIT-ICAI and Compass Lexecon shifts responsibility towards specific operational decisions by the System Operator. The central thesis challenges the <GlossaryLink term="Mallado">meshing (_mallado_</GlossaryLink>) manoeuvre executed by Redeia, arguing that it increased the capacitive contribution of the network under conditions of low synchronous strength, generating an overvoltage pulse amplified by the operator's limited observability of the 220 kV network — a phenomenon termed <GlossaryLink term="Tap-Lag">Tap-Lag</GlossaryLink>.

**European system manager narrative (<GlossaryLink term="ENTSO-E">ENTSO-E</GlossaryLink>).** ENTSO-E's factual report adopts a continental synchronous area perspective, analysing the propagation of <GlossaryLink term="Oscilaciones electromecánicas">inter-area oscillations</GlossaryLink> and questioning the adequacy of the <GlossaryLink term="Criterio N-1">*N*−1 Security Criterion</GlossaryLink> against the ultra-fast dynamic phenomena characteristic of power-electronics- dominated systems.

_[Figure: Collapse sequence for 28 April, highlighting the interaction between Operator Actions (OA) and Automatic Protection Actions (AA). Source: Albustami et al., 2025.]_

The scope of the work is defined by the systematic contrast of these three narratives against European <GlossaryLink term="NC RfG">Network Codes — particularly the RfG Regulation</GlossaryLink> — and against modern principles of <GlossaryLink term="Estabilidad de tensión">voltage stability</GlossaryLink> in inverter-dominated systems, with the aim of identifying structural lessons, methodological limitations and regulatory gaps that condition the resilience of the European power system during its decarbonisation transition.

## The structural vulnerability of the Iberian system

The energy transition has profoundly and irreversibly altered the dynamics of the peninsular system. The accelerated displacement of synchronous generation by IBR reduces the rotating masses coupled to the network and, with them, two critical parameters simultaneously: system <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia (_H_</GlossaryLink>) — which fell to zonal values of **1.3 s to 1.8 s** in the southern peninsula, below the 2 s threshold that the technical literature associates with compromised dynamic margins — and short-circuit power available at network nodes. This dual reduction does not only affect the frequency–active power axis: it critically undermines the system's ability to dynamically absorb reactive power and sustain voltage under fast transients, which is precisely the axis on which the 28 April collapse unfolded.

_[Figure: Electromechanical metamorphosis of the power system. The displacement of large rotating masses by IBR resources drastically reduces total system inertia. Source: FutuRed, 2024.]_

On the morning of the incident, the system was operating with **82% non-synchronous renewable penetration** under valley demand conditions. To balance generation, Redeia decoupled virtually all available thermal units, reducing the number of online synchronous generators to historical lows. The IBR fleet was operating almost entirely in <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">grid-following</GlossaryLink> mode: inverters that require a stable external voltage and frequency as a synchronisation reference. With synchronous units removed from the dispatch, the system lost its primary mechanism for dynamic reactive power absorption and, with it, the natural damping against voltage transients.

Added to this internal fragility is the structural isolation of the Iberian Peninsula. With a cross-border interconnection capacity of **7.9%** relative to peak demand — well below EU targets — the Iberian system cannot import dynamic stability from the continental core in emergency conditions. The convergence of low synchronous strength and relative network isolation created the vulnerability that made it impossible to arrest the overvoltage cascade at **12:33:24 CEST**.

## Methodology: forensic triangulation and cross-validation

The research adopts a comparative forensic analysis approach, structured in three phases:

**1. Primary source compilation.** Systematic study of official expert reports, underpinned by the processing of more than **170 GB of technical records** from the System Operator, including <GlossaryLink term="SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)">SCADA</GlossaryLink> telemetry and oscillographic records.

**2. Narrative triangulation.** Systematic comparison of the government thesis against the generator sector's expert analysis, focused on the two main axes of disagreement: the management of the meshing manoeuvre and the observability limitations of the 220 kV network.

**3. Cross-technical validation.** Comparison of chronological sequences against the NREL's independent inter-area oscillation analysis, derived from <GlossaryLink term="PMU (Phasor Measurement Unit)">Phasor Measurement Unit (_PMU_</GlossaryLink>) data with continental coverage — recordings whose institutional independence allows arbitration between conflicting accounts.

_[Figure: Topological location of PMU sensors in the European synchronous system. Coverage density proved critical for inter-area oscillation verification. Source: NREL.]_

Large language models were employed as documentary assistance tools — classification, synthesis and critical data extraction — given the exceptional volume of technical documentation generated after the incident. Their application was strictly limited to document organisation: these models have known limitations when handling fast-dynamic phenomena such as the _Tap-Lag_ mechanism or the <GlossaryLink term="UFLS (Underfrequency Load Shedding)">UFLS</GlossaryLink> paradox. Technical comparison, causal evaluation and the conclusions of this work remain the sole responsibility of the author's judgement.
