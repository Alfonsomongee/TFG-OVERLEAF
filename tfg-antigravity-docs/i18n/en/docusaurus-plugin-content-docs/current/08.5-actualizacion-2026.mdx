## 1. The "Overvoltage-Driven Blackout" Paradigm

Initial analyses suggested a classic collapse due to lack of inertia or frequency deviation. However, the independent study by the Institute for Technological Research (IIT) of Comillas Pontifical University — peer-reviewed in *ScienceDirect* (Rouco, Lobato, Echavarren, 2026) — has introduced an unprecedented technical concept: the overvoltage-driven blackout.

:::warning [Technical Revelation]
The fatal sequence was not a widespread voltage drop. When the protection logics activated, the grid suffered a cascade of massive overvoltage trips. The disconnection of load and the enormous injection of capacitive energy from the 400 kV lines (without sufficient reactors connected) raised voltages above the limit of generation protections. The trip of the 400/220 kV transformer in Granada at 12:32:57, which lost 355 MW, was the trigger for the cascade.
:::

According to the forensic reconstruction, Grid-Following inverter-connected plants operating in fixed power factor mode reacted to active power oscillations by injecting enormous ramps of reactive power. Faced with a weakened grid (with a low Short-Circuit Ratio), the Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) of these inverters caused instabilities that the system was unable to dampen.

## 2. Operational Opacity and the Role of HVDC

One of the most controversial points of the investigation has been the performance of the system operator (REE). In July 2025, the independent consortium (AELEC, INESC TEC, Compass Lexecon) denounced a severe lack of transparency regarding data from state estimators and Phasor Measurement Units (PMUs).

The critical role of the HVDC INELFE-1 mode change to PMODE1 and its consequences on dynamic support capability are analyzed in detail in Chapters [2](./02-context.mdx) and [3](./03-incident-analysis.mdx).

## 3. Institutional Consequences and Multi-Million Dollar Lawsuits

The blackout not only broke the electrical system but also the institutional relationships within the sector, triggering an unprecedented judicial avalanche:

- CNMC Files: Since April 2025, 66 sanctioning proceedings have been initiated. REE and the company owning the Almaraz power plant face "very serious" charges (up to €60M). Concurrently, eleven producers (including Endesa, Iberdrola, and Mercuria Solar) face "serious" charges for non-compliance with grid codes.
- Massive Civil Lawsuits: As the statute of limitations expired in April 2026, industrial giants like Repsol have filed lawsuits against the system for €125 million in lost profits and damages to petrochemical complexes. Iryo and Moeve have also notified their intention to sue.
- Classified Audio: Hearings of the Senate Investigation Commission have made public audio from the REE control room. In them, operators attributed the severe oscillations at 12:00 to the *"poor management"* of the photovoltaic plant operators. However, the CNMC clarified that the 0.6 Hz oscillation was caused by a defect in the control loop of the large photovoltaic plant in Badajoz, not by human "poor management." This reveals that the risk was imminent minutes before the final collapse, even though the initial diagnosis in the control room was erroneous.

### Iberdrola vs. Redeia Litigation

On April 21, 2026, the Commercial Court No. 15 of Madrid (Judge Teodoro Ladrón) rejected the jurisdictional challenge filed by REE and Redeia, confirming the jurisdiction of civil courts to hear Iberdrola's lawsuit for unfair competition. The order delimits that the proceeding will not judge the technical causes of the blackout — the exclusive competence of the CNMC — but rather the corporate conduct and public statements of Redeia's management that, according to Iberdrola, damaged its reputation and stock market value.

## 4. The Regulatory Response: The Grid-Forming Mandate

Given the evidence of the structural failure, ENTSO-E published its long-awaited "Phase II Report" in November 2025, laying the groundwork for the new European grid code (NC RfG 2.0).

The technical specifications of NC RfG 2.0 and its mandatory Grid-Forming mandate are detailed in [Chapter 7](./07-future-resilience.mdx). What is relevant for the 2026 update is the implementation status:

Despite the Iberian collapse, the official adoption of NC RfG 2.0 by the European Commission has been deprioritized until late 2026. However, countries like the United Kingdom (Stability Pathfinder project) and Spain have accelerated on their own.

### The Iberian Storage Boom
The systemic cost of maintaining the system reinforced with gas after the blackout amounted to an additional €666 million in just ten months. As a direct reaction, the Spanish battery market experienced unprecedented acceleration: installed BESS (Battery Energy Storage Systems) capacity increased by 589%, from a symbolic 28 MW to approximately 193 MW operational in a single year, driving a native grid architecture for Black Start services.

## Conclusion of the Crisis

In retrospect, the "2025 blackout" is no longer studied in 2026 as a fortuitous accident derived from hydraulic drought or solar excess. It is the first documented example of a collapse precipitated by the structural inadequacy of the prevailing regulatory-technical framework to the physics of high renewable penetration systems. The Iberian Peninsula forcibly became the living laboratory that forced Europe to rewrite the rules of electrical stability for the next decade.
