<GlitchTitle>2026 Update: One Year Later</GlitchTitle>

The blackout of April 28, 2025 marked a systemic turning point. As of May 2026, the final reports from <GlossaryLink term="ENTSO-E">ENTSO-E</GlossaryLink> (published on March 20, 2026) and independent forensic investigations have brought to light a series of technical and regulatory revelations that drastically alter the initial narrative of the incident.

## 1. The "Overvoltage-Driven Blackout" Paradigm

Initial analyses suggested a classic collapse due to lack of inertia or frequency deviation. However, the independent study by the Institute for Research in Technology (IIT) at Universidad Pontificia Comillas —peer-reviewed in *ScienceDirect* (Rouco, Lobato, Echavarren, 2026)— has introduced an unprecedented technical concept: the **overvoltage-driven blackout**.

:::warning [Technical Revelation]
The fatal sequence was not a generalized voltage drop. Upon activation of the protection logics, the grid suffered a cascade of massive overvoltage trips. The load shedding and the enormous injection of capacitive energy from the 400 kV lines (without sufficient connected reactors) raised voltages above the generation protection limits. The trip of the 400/220 kV transformer in Granada at 12:32:57, which lost 355 MW, was the trigger for the cascade.
:::

According to the forensic reconstruction, the plants connected via <GlossaryLink term="Grid-Following (GFL)">Grid-Following</GlossaryLink> inverters operating in **fixed power factor** mode reacted to active power oscillations by injecting enormous reactive power ramps. In a weakened grid (with a low <GlossaryLink term="Short-Circuit Ratio (SCR)">Short-Circuit Ratio</GlossaryLink>), the <GlossaryLink term="Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)">PLL</GlossaryLink> of these inverters caused instabilities that the system was unable to dampen.

## 2. Operational Opacity and the Role of HVDC

One of the most controversial points of the investigation has been the actions of the system operator (<GlossaryLink term="Red Eléctrica de España (REE)">REE</GlossaryLink>). In July 2025, the independent consortium (AELEC, INESC TEC, Compass Lexecon) denounced a severe lack of transparency regarding the data from state estimators and Phasor Measurement Units (<GlossaryLink term="PMU">PMU</GlossaryLink>). 

The most critical finding of the investigation commissions reveals that, just minutes before the blackout, REE modified the operating mode of the INELFE <GlossaryLink term="HVDC">HVDC</GlossaryLink> link between Spain and France. The link went from its "AC emulation" mode to constant DC power control, neutralizing its ability to dampen the lethal 0.6 Hz oscillation.

## 3. Institutional Consequences and Multimillion-Dollar Litigation

The blackout not only broke the electrical system, but also the institutional relations of the sector, sparking an unprecedented judicial avalanche:

- **CNMC Proceedings**: Since April 2025, 66 disciplinary proceedings have been initiated. REE and Almaraz face "very serious" charges (up to €60M). Concurrently, eleven generation companies (including Endesa, Iberdrola, and Mercuria Solar) face "serious" charges for non-compliance with grid codes.
- **Massive Civil Lawsuits**: Upon expiration of the statute of limitations in April 2026, industrial giants like **Repsol** have formally filed lawsuits against the system for **125 million euros** in lost profits and damages to petrochemical complexes. Iryo and Moeve have also notified their intention to sue.
- **Classified Audios**: The hearings of the Senate Investigation Commission have made public audios from the REE control room. In them, the operators attributed the severe oscillations and voltage instabilities at 12:00 to *"the poor management of a high-power photovoltaic plant in Badajoz"*, revealing that the risk was imminent minutes before the final collapse.

## 4. The Regulatory Response: The Grid-Forming Mandate

Faced with the evidence of structural failure, <GlossaryLink term="ENTSO-E">ENTSO-E</GlossaryLink> published its awaited "Phase II Report" in November 2025, laying the foundations for the new European grid code (NC RfG 2.0).

:::info [The New GFM Standard]
The regulatory draft mandates <GlossaryLink term="Grid-Forming (GFM)">Grid-Forming</GlossaryLink> technology for all new non-synchronous plants and storage systems larger than **1 MW**. It requires a current response time of < 10 ms, ride-through against phase angle jumps, and the provision of synthetic inertia equivalent to that of a synchronous machine.
:::

Despite the Iberian collapse, the official adoption of NC RfG 2.0 by the European Commission has been deprioritized until late 2026. However, countries like the UK (*Stability Pathfinder* project) and Spain have accelerated on their own.

### The Iberian Storage Boom
The systemic cost of keeping the system reinforced with gas after the blackout amounted to an additional **666 million euros** in just ten months. As a direct reaction, the Spanish battery market experienced an "electroshock": the installed BESS (Battery Energy Storage Systems) capacity has **multiplied by 589%**, going from a symbolic 28 MW to almost 200 MW operational in a single year, driving a native grid architecture for <GlossaryLink term="Black Start">Black Start</GlossaryLink> services.

## Conclusion of the Crisis

In retrospect, the "2025 blackout" is no longer studied in 2026 as a fortuitous accident derived from the hydraulic drought or the solar surplus. It is the first documented exponent of a collapse caused by regulatory strangulation and a technological transition that removed synchronous machines before deploying their electronic equivalents. The Iberian Peninsula became by force the living laboratory that has forced Europe to rewrite the rules of electrical stability for the next decade.
