  <AnnexKeyQuestion>
    What equations, models, and simulations allow for the quantitative reproduction of the physical, financial, and communicative phenomena that converged in the blackout of April 28?
  </AnnexKeyQuestion>

  <AnnexThesisBox>
    The collapse of April 28 was a multiscale phenomenon governed by the physical laws of electrical engineering, marginalist market incentives, and social dynamics of information propagation. This annex brings together the interactive simulators that model these behaviors quantitatively, allowing independent variables to be varied to analyze grid stability, deviation costs, the collapse of the power flow Jacobian, or the delay in institutional communication.
  </AnnexThesisBox>

  <AnnexBlackoutRelevance>
    This annex provides the formulation support and interactive models that quantitatively validate the empirical analysis developed in the chapters and factual annexes of the TFG.
  </AnnexBlackoutRelevance>

  <AnnexMethodNote type="reconstruccion">
    This annex isolates and groups all the interactive tools in the document that are not based on static historical data, but on mathematical, financial, and electrical physics equations. The user can alter the independent variables of the formulas to observe the dynamic behavior of the resulting curves.
  </AnnexMethodNote>

  <em>The annex is organized into five sections covering the different mathematical domains of the study: grid strength and voltage collapse, reactive power and electromagnetic transients, frequency control and inertia, market and grid economics, and system dynamics models.</em>
