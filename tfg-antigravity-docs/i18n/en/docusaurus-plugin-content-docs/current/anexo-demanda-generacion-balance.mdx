  <AnnexKeyQuestion>
    How did the forecast demand, generation mix, and operational balance of the peninsular system condition the available dynamic safety margin on the morning of April 28, 2025?
  </AnnexKeyQuestion>

  <AnnexThesisBox>
    On the morning of April 28, peninsular generation covered demand comfortably, but the composition of the mix — 82% inverter-based sources, only 11 synchronous units connected — left the system with the lowest inertia of the year and a short-circuit power in the south of the peninsula below safe operating thresholds. Demand forecasts did not anticipate exceptional conditions; the forecast-to-actual deviation was less than 5%. The vulnerability lay not in the amount of available power but in the dynamic properties of that power. This annex establishes the necessary — though not sufficient — condition for the subsequent collapse.
  </AnnexThesisBox>

  <AnnexBlackoutRelevance>
    This annex documents the prior operating conditions that reduced the peninsular system's inertia and short-circuit power below safety thresholds, creating the precondition for the April 28 blackout.
  </AnnexBlackoutRelevance>

  <AnnexMethodNote type="reconstruccion">
    The documentary evidence in this annex is organized to facilitate an integrated reading of the evolution of demand and the generation balance during the incident.
  </AnnexMethodNote>

  <em>This annex is organized into four sections. The first presents the operational condition of April 28: the generation profile, connected units, and the mix at 12:30 CEST. The second contrasts demand forecasts with reality, demonstrating that the vulnerability was invisible from conventional signals. The third gathers medium-term indicators as structural context. The fourth places the event within the Iberian energy transition.</em>

  <AnnexSection id="previsiones-demanda" eyebrow="Section 2" title="Demand Forecasts: Absence of Alarm Signals">
    The D-2, D-1, and 8:00h forecasts for Spain did not anticipate any exceptional condition.
    <AnnexEvidence type="table" id="spanish-demand-forecast" level={2} />
    Forecast-to-actual deviation less than 5% — demand was not the problem.
    Portuguese forecasts confirm the same pattern of normality.
    <AnnexEvidence type="table" id="portuguese-demand-forecast" level={2} />
    The ESIOS series of peninsular demand with 5-minute resolution is the system's electrocardiogram: stable demand until 12:33 CEST, a vertical drop to 0 MW, and 19 hours of recovery.
    <AnnexEvidence type="chart" id="chart-1" level={2} />
    The combined ES+PT load confirms that the collapse was simultaneous in both countries — there was no appreciable lag between the Spanish and Portuguese drops.
    <AnnexEvidence type="chart" id="chart-2" level={2} />
    The P48 production program was instantly canceled at 12:33 CEST. The estimated energy not supplied (150,000–180,000 MWh) multiplies several times the daily programmed volume (27,812 MWh).
    <AnnexEvidence type="chart" id="chart-3" level={2} />
    The normality of the forecasts is precisely the evidence: the vulnerability of April 28 was invisible to conventional demand signals.
  </AnnexSection>

  <AnnexSection id="capacidad-indicadores" eyebrow="Section 3" title="Capacity, Reserves, and Medium-Term Indicators">
    The peninsular fleet had more than 121 GW of installed capacity, of which ~61 GW was conventional synchronous. At noon on April 28, only 7 GW were dispatched. The gap between installed capacity and actual generation is the footprint of the merit order.
    <AnnexEvidenceGrid>
      <AnnexEvidence type="figure" id="capacidad_instalada_2025" level={3} />
      <AnnexEvidence type="chart" id="chart-5" level={3} />
    </AnnexEvidenceGrid>
    With 15 TWh stored in week 18, Iberian reservoirs were near their historical maximum. This reserve allowed the hydraulic Black Start to be viable in hours, not days.
    <AnnexEvidence type="chart" id="chart-7" level={3} />
    The CO₂-free generation indicator reached 91.2% at 11:30 CEST — the 2025 maximum on a working day. The system collapsed at its point of maximum decarbonization.
    <AnnexEvidence type="chart" id="chart-8" level={3} />
    The disconnection of ~700 MW of distributed self-consumption not observable by REE's SCADA added a layer of operational uncertainty to the demand-generation balance.
