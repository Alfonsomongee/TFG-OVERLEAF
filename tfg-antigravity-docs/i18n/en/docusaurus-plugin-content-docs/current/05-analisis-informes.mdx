# Comparative Analysis of Technical Reports (Phase 5)

The Iberian blackout of April 28, 2025 generated technical documentation unprecedented in European electrical system history. In the months following the incident, four institutional actors published technical reports with partially contradictory conclusions about the collapse causes.

This chapter confronts the four technical narratives to identify both methodological consensus and irreconcilable divergences, situating the debate in its fundamental physical axes.

---

## Government and REE Vision

The Government-appointed Technical Commission report, published in collaboration with Red Eléctrica de España, attributes the collapse to **multifactorial causality** with three simultaneous factors:

1. **Generalized regulatory non-compliance of the <GlossaryLink term="IBR">IBR</GlossaryLink> fleet**: Analysis of <GlossaryLink term="SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)">SCADA</GlossaryLink> records determined that **22% of photovoltaic inverters** operated outside reactive voltage and power factor ranges required by P.O. 7.4. This systematic deviation compromised the system's global capacity to absorb voltage disturbances.

2. **Dynamic management of peninsular meshing**: The network configuration adopted April 28 was analyzed as an aggravating factor reducing topological robustness against multiple contingencies.

3. **Cascade of overvoltage protection failure**: The tripping sequence is attributed to correct but cascaded operation of overvoltage protection relays, whose settings did not account for collective dynamics of low-inertia systems.

---

## Redeia Vision

Redeia's technical report adopted a more defensive posture, demonstrating that network configuration complied with the <GlossaryLink term="Criterio N-1">*N-1 Criterion*</GlossaryLink> and operational decisions violated no procedures.

Redeia points directly to **non-compliance of <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">reactive power</GlossaryLink> curves** by <GlossaryLink term="IBR">IBR</GlossaryLink> generators as the primary trigger, documenting that 847 of 3,872 registered inverters operated outside contractual limits.

---

## Generator Sector Vision (IIT-ICAI / AELEC)

The most technically dense analysis was published by the **Instituto de Investigación Tecnológica (IIT-ICAI)** in collaboration with **AELEC**. This report proposes **physically distinct causality**: collapse resulted from **<GlossaryLink term="Colapso Q-V">voltage instability of fundamentally capacitive nature</GlossaryLink>** generated by network configuration itself.

### The Ferranti Effect and Capacitive Injection from Meshing

The central argument articulates around the <GlossaryLink term="Efecto Ferranti">**Ferranti effect**</GlossaryLink>: lightly-loaded long lines act as distributed capacitors and elevate voltage. The report quantifies that meshing injected between **1.05 and 2.4 GVAr** of <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">capacitive reactive power</GlossaryLink>, generating overvoltage pressures exceeding system absorption capacity.

### Contraction of Q-V Margin at Carmona

Analysis of the <GlossaryLink term="Colapso Q-V">Q-V</GlossaryLink> curve at Carmona 400 kV node documents dramatic contraction of voltage stability margin by **57.3%** relative to normal conditions.

### The Tap-Lag Phenomenon and Observability Gap

The most novel technical contribution is identification of <GlossaryLink term="Tap-Lag">**"Tap-Lag"**</GlossaryLink> as an amplifying factor. <GlossaryLink term="OLTC (On-Load Tap Changer)">**OLTC transformers**</GlossaryLink> adjust with tens-of-seconds delays, creating an **observability gap**: <GlossaryLink term="SCADA (Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition)">SCADA</GlossaryLink> showed 418 kV at Carmona while real 220 kV voltage had collapsed to 244 kV.

---

## ENTSO-E Vision

The European Transmission System Operators Network report adopts a larger-scale perspective. Its central argument is the **electromechanical insufficiency of <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">*grid-following*</GlossaryLink> inverters** to sustain high-penetration renewable systems under severe disturbances.

<GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">GFL</GlossaryLink> inverters, representing approximately **78% of installed capacity** on April 28, operate via a <GlossaryLink term="Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)">PLL (*Phase-Locked Loop*)</GlossaryLink> needing to "read" stable networks. <GlossaryLink term="GFM (Grid-Forming)">**Grid-forming (GFM)**</GlossaryLink> inverters can actively impose voltage waveforms, behaving electromechanically like conventional synchronous generators.

The central recommendation is revision of **Network Code on Requirements for Generators (<GlossaryLink term="NC RfG">NC RfG</GlossaryLink>)** to mandate <GlossaryLink term="GFM (Grid-Forming)">GFM</GlossaryLink> mode for <GlossaryLink term="IBR">IBR</GlossaryLink> generators in networks with renewable penetration exceeding 60%.

## Comparative Table: Consensus and Divergences

### Points of Consensus

All reports agree:
- Collapse was primarily **voltage**, not frequency
- Protection relays operated **technically correctly**
- Current regulations are **insufficient** for systems with high <GlossaryLink term="IBR">IBR</GlossaryLink> penetration
- **<GlossaryLink term="GFM (Grid-Forming)">Grid-forming</GlossaryLink>** generation and <GlossaryLink term="PMU (Phasor Measurement Unit)">PMU</GlossaryLink> coverage needed
