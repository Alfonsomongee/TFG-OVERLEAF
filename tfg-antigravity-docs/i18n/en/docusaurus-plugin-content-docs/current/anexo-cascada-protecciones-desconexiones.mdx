  <AnnexKeyQuestion>
    How did the cascade of disconnections propagate from the root trip in the southwestern zone to the total collapse of the Iberian system, and which protections acted at each phase?
  </AnnexKeyQuestion>

  <AnnexThesisBox>
    The April 28 cascade unfolded in four phases compressed into less than five minutes: (1) propagation of overvoltages through the southwestern 400 kV network, amplified by the Ferranti effect and the Tap-Lag of regulating transformers; (2) massive tripping of ANSI 59 protections in IBR inverters with a loss of ~4.5 GW in 11 seconds; (3) activation of automatic defense mechanisms — UFLS load shedding and pumping disconnection — which proved insufficient given the speed of the disturbance; (4) frequency collapse and total loss of peninsular synchronism. The protections operated as specified; the specification did not contemplate a scenario of widespread overvoltage with low inertia.
  </AnnexThesisBox>

  <AnnexBlackoutRelevance>
