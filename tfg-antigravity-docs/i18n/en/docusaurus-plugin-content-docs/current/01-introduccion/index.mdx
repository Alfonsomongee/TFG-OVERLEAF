# Introduction

## Object and Scope

The primary object of this Final Degree Project (TFG) consists of critical and comparative study of technical, regulatory, and operational narratives formulated by various electrical sector actors, based on information and reports published in response to the **Voltage Zero** (_blackout_) event that occurred in the Iberian synchronous system on April 28, 2025.

This event involved sudden loss of more than **15 GW of generation** in mere seconds and left approximately **60 million people** without electrical supply, constituting one of the episodes of systemic instability of greatest magnitude recorded in continental Europe in recent decades.

The exceptional nature of the collapse—characterized by cascade of disconnections associated with overvoltage phenomena in context of high penetration of <GlossaryLink term="IBR">_Inverter-Based Resources_ (IBR)</GlossaryLink>—generated profound interpretive disagreement regarding its root causes and assignment of technical and operational responsibilities.

### Three Institutional Narratives

This investigation focuses on critical examination of divergences and consensus points existing between three predominant institutional visions:

#### Administration Vision (Government and Redeia)

The report of the National Security Council Analysis Committee and the System Operator articulates interpretation grounded in regulatory compliance and technical discipline of generation fleet. According to this perspective, the system possessed sufficient regulatory tools to mitigate the transient, attributing event aggravation to insufficient absorption of <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">inductive reactive power</GlossaryLink>—a deficit that prevented containing voltage escalation—as established in Operating Procedure **P.O. 7.4**.

#### Generation Sector Vision (ICAI/AELEC Report)

The independent technical report elaborated by IIT-ICAI and Compass Lexecon locates root cause in concrete operational decisions adopted by System Operator. This narrative questions meshing maneuver executed by Redeia, arguing it significantly increased network capacitive contribution in scenario of low <GlossaryLink term="Potencia de cortocircuito">synchronous strength</GlossaryLink>, generating overvoltage pulse aggravated by operator observability limitations over 220 kV network (<GlossaryLink term="Tap-Lag">Tap-Lag phenomenon</GlossaryLink>).

#### European Manager Vision (ENTSO-E)

The factual report published by ENTSO-E introduces perspective oriented to continental synchronous area stability, analyzing propagation of inter-area oscillations and indicating limitations of traditional static security approaches based on _N-1 Criterion_ against ultrafast dynamic phenomena characteristic of systems with high power-electronics-based generation penetration.

![Replication of collapse sequence](/figuras/albustami_ieee39_secuencia.png)

The scope of this TFG is circumscribed to technical scrutiny of these argumentative frameworks through their contrast with European network codes (_Network Codes_, especially RfG Regulation) and with modern principles of stability in electrical systems dominated by inverters.

## Technical Justification: Iberian System Vulnerability

Transition toward decarbonized generation model has structurally transformed peninsular system dynamics. Accelerated replacement of conventional synchronous plants by Inverter-Based Resources reduces rotating masses coupled to network and, with them, both system <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">**inertia** ($H$)</GlossaryLink>—which fell to zonal values between 1.3 s and 1.8 s in peninsular south, below 2 s threshold that technical literature associates with compromised dynamic margins—and available **short-circuit power** at nodes.

This dual reduction does not affect only frequency–active power axis: it weakens also, and critically, system capacity to dynamically absorb reactive power ($Q$) and sustain voltage against fast transients, which is precisely the axis on which April 28 collapse was articulated.

This structural vulnerability manifested extremely on April 28, 2025. Morning of incident, system operated with **82% non-synchronous renewable penetration**, in context of _valley demand_ that reduced load in transport corridors and exacerbated <GlossaryLink term="Efecto Ferranti">capacitive effect</GlossaryLink> of 400 kV lines. To balance generation, operator decoupled practically entire available thermal units, leaving synchronous generators at historical minimums.

![Electromechanical metamorphosis of power system](/figuras/futured_grid_evolution.png)

Operating under "light grid" paradigm contracts stability margins nonlinearly. Unlike synchronous alternators, IBR fleet operates practically entirely in <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">grid-following</GlossaryLink> mode: their inverters need stable external voltage ($V$) and frequency ($f$) as synchronism reference. With synchronous units drastically reduced, system lost its principal mechanism of dynamic reactive power absorption, eliminating natural damping against voltage transients.

To this internal fragility adds structural condition of Iberian Peninsula as **"energy island"**: its interconnection capacity with France sits far below community directives, preventing import of dynamic stability from continental core. Convergence of both factors—low synchronous strength and relative network isolation—configured vulnerability making impossible to brake overvoltage cascade.

## Research Methodology

This investigation's methodology is based on **comparative forensic analysis** oriented to reconstruct April 28, 2025 event sequence through triangulation of divergent primary sources. The process was articulated in three methodological phases:

1. **Primary source compilation**: Systematic study of official technical reports, which are themselves sustained in processing of over 170 GB of technical records by System Operator, including SCADA telemetry and oscillographic records.

2. **Narrative triangulation**: Systematic contrast of Administration thesis versus generation sector technical analysis regarding meshing management and 220 kV network inobservability.

3. **Cross-technical validation**: Collation of chronological sequences from each report with independent inter-area oscillation analysis published by NREL based on <GlossaryLink term="PMU (Phasor Measurement Unit)">Phasor Measurement Unit (PMU)</GlossaryLink> data, whose continental coverage permits arbitration between divergent versions.

![Topological location of Phasor Measurement Units (PMUs) in Europe](/figuras/pmu_sensors_europe.png)

Given volume of technical documentation generated after incident, large-scale language models were employed as documentary assistance tool. Their application focused on report classification and synthesis, extraction of critical data, and identification of recurrent failure patterns between different sources.

Nevertheless, use of these tools was strictly circumscribed to documentary organization and synthesis. Language models present known limitations against fast-dynamics phenomena—such as interpretation of <GlossaryLink term="UFLS (Underfrequency Load Shedding)">UFLS paradox</GlossaryLink> or Tap-Lag mechanism—so technical comparison, causality assessment, and work conclusions are exclusive responsibility of author's judgment.
