# Technical Analysis: The Frequency Collapse in 27 Seconds

## The Overvoltage-Driven Blackout

Unlike traditional frequency collapses, the April 28 event was an **overvoltage-driven collapse** — a rare but devastating category of cascading failure.

### Context: The Swing Equation

The frequency of an electrical system is governed by the active power balance:

$$\frac{d\omega}{dt} = \frac{\omega_0}{2H} (P_m - P_e)$$

Where:
- $H$ = <GlossaryLink term="System Inertia (H)" /> (seconds)
- $\omega_0$ = Nominal frequency (50 Hz)
- $P_m$ = Injected mechanical power
- $P_e$ = Electrical power extracted by the load

The **ROCOF** (Rate of Change of Frequency) is:

$$\text{ROCOF} = \frac{\Delta P}{2H \times S_{base}}$$

On April 28, with $H = 2.45$ seconds (critically low due to 78% renewable penetration) and $\Delta P = 2,200$ MW, the initial ROCOF was close to 0.014 Hz/s, but the true dynamics were multidimensional.

## The Three Phases of the Collapse

### Phase 1: Forced Oscillations (12:03 — 12:32 CEST)

For almost half an hour, operators at the Red Eléctrica control center observed anomalous oscillations in the southern zone:

- **Local oscillation at 0.63 Hz**: Caused by instability of the PLL (Phase-Locked Loop) controls of grid-following inverters in a <GlossaryLink term="Weak Grid" />
- **Inter-area oscillation at 0.21 Hz**: The Iberian Peninsula swaying against the continental European grid

These oscillatory modes signaled a structural vulnerability: the southern grid operated without enough local synchronous generation to provide damping and short-circuit support.

### Phase 2: Fast Frequency Sag (12:32:57 — 12:33:18 CEST)

The sudden loss of 2,200 MW of renewable generation in Granada, Badajoz, and Seville precipitated a frequency drop to ROCOF = 0.90 Hz/s.

**Critical paradox:** In a strong grid system, the loss of generation causes **underfrequency and voltage drop**. On April 28, it resulted in **overvoltage**.

Why? The solar plants that disconnected had been actively absorbing <GlossaryLink term="Reactive Power (Q)" /> to damp the region. When they disappeared, the reactive power sink also disappeared. Simultaneously, the 2,200 MW loss of active power caused power to flow backward through long-haul transmission lines, which acted as massive capacitors (Ferranti effect), injecting uncompensated reactive power.

### Phase 3: The Cascading Collapse (12:33:18 — 12:33:24 CEST)

Upon reaching 49.0 Hz, the <GlossaryLink term="UFLS (Under-Frequency Load Shedding)" /> relays automatically activated, disconnecting approximately 3,100 MW of load.

<br />

:::warning UFLS PROTECTIONS CASCADE
The Iberian under-frequency load shedding system is designed to save the grid through staggered demand disconnection. Paradoxically, the perfect execution of Stage 1 caused the overvoltage that triggered the blackout.
:::

**The lethal blow:** The demand reduction lowered the currents in the lines. Lower currents meant lower inductive voltage drops ($I^2X$), allowing the parasitic capacitance of the lines to completely dominate. The voltage spiked to 435+ kV.

The overvoltage protections in the solar inverters tripped in a cascade, disconnecting thousands of additional megawatts in a matter of seconds. Legacy distance protection relays, unable to interpret the distorted fault signatures of inverter-based resources (IBR), misinterpreted the chaos as a massive, internal short circuit.

At 12:33:21, the distance relays opened the AC interconnection lines with France. Three seconds later, the HVDC link disconnected. The Iberian synchronous system collapsed.

## Why It Was Inevitable

Under these extreme conditions (H = 2.21–2.71 s, 78% renewables, weak grid in the south), **there was no way to stop the collapse once initiated**.

### 1. Insufficient Inertia

The 3,300 MW of nuclear generation could not provide enough primary frequency control against a 2,200 MW deficit when $H < 3$ seconds.

Theoretical primary frequency control available:

$$\Delta f_{recovered} = -\frac{\Delta P}{f_0} \times \frac{K_p}{1 + sT_{ag}}$$

With $K_p \approx 10$ and $T_{ag} = 30$ ms (governor action time), the response was too slow. The ROCOF exceeded protection thresholds before rotational inertia could stabilize it.

### 2. ROCOF-Triggered Protections

Once ROCOF exceeded 1.0 Hz/s, the remaining synchronous machines automatically executed self-protection trips to prevent shaft damage from polar slip.

This was the **point of no return**. By losing nuclear generation, the system lost its only stabilizing anchor. Without inertia, without reactive power, without short-circuit support, the grid collapsed in a cascade.

### 3. Architecture Flaw: Legacy Protections vs. IBR

The deep root was an **architecture mismatch between 40-year-old distance relay protection and a 78% power-electronics-based grid**.

Distance relays calculate apparent impedance:

$$Z = \frac{V}{I}$$

Under fault conditions, they measure $Z$ to determine if the event is inside or outside their protection zone.

**The problem:** The limited <GlossaryLink term="Short-Circuit Ratio (SCR)" /> of IBRs means the fault current is very low. Distance relays receive **distorted and low-magnitude** fault signals, which they cannot interpret correctly.

The ENTSO-E expert panel concluded that **the relays misinterpreted the electrical chaos as a massive internal short circuit** and opened the interconnection lines with France — the exact action that ensured the total collapse.

## Regulatory and Market Context

The event exposed a deep regulatory flaw: **market frameworks were not designed for a grid with 78% renewable penetration**.

Although many utility-scale solar plants had certified capabilities for dynamic voltage support and <GlossaryLink term="Reactive Power (Q)" /> injection, existing market mechanisms did not incentivize (and even legally prohibited) them from providing these services in real time. The grid operator could not "dispatch" reactive power locally in the south because it lacked a market for it.

**Result:** When the southern region desperately needed a local reactive power sink, there was none available.

## Restoration: The 7-Zone Black Start Operation

After the total collapse at 12:33:24, Red Eléctrica executed its national defense and restoration plan. Rebuilding a 31 GW system from "scratch" required 16 hours of delicate operation:

1. **12:44** — Re-energization of the first AC line to France (11 minutes after the collapse)
2. **13:04** — Reconnection to Morocco
3. **13:30** — Start of black start sequences of Spanish hydroelectric plants
4. **16:11** — First Portuguese black start
5. **22:33** — Restoration of 50% of public demand
6. **04:00 (April 29)** — System completely restored (16 hours later)

## Technical Lessons

This event is a turning point for global power systems:

1. **Low inertia is existential**: With $H < 3$ seconds, collapses can occur in < 30 seconds, faster than human intervention.
2. **Legacy protections cannot handle IBR-based grids**: A fundamental architectural overhaul is required.
3. **Overvoltage is as dangerous as underfrequency**: Protection schemes must guard against both.
4. **The market must prioritize stability over price**: Grid services cannot be optional.
