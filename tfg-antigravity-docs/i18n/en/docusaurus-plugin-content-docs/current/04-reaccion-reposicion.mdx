# Operator Response and Supply Restoration (Phase 4)

## Emergency Management by Red Eléctrica

Following the absolute **voltage collapse** at 12:33:30 CEST, the peninsular electrical system transitioned from an uncontrollable dynamic crisis to a phase of emergency management and structural restoration. The magnitude of the collapse—loss of more than 15 GW and total disconnection from the Continental synchronous system—forced the System Operator (REE) to abandon steady-state operation logic dictated by Procedure 1.1 and activate emergency protocols provided in **Procedure 1.6** (P.O. 1.6).

![Official timeline of collapse and restoration phases](/figuras/cronograma_fases_gobierno.png)

Procedure 1.1 establishes functioning and safety criteria for steady-state system operation, grounded in compliance with the <GlossaryLink term="Criterio N-1">*N-1 Criterion*</GlossaryLink> and maintenance of voltages between 380 kV and 435 kV in the transmission network. The April 28 cascade demonstrated limitations of static validation of these criteria against ultrafast dynamic transients in low-inertia networks.

The institutional response was immediate and coordinated across multiple simultaneous fronts:

- **12:34 CEST** — REE contacted its Portuguese counterpart (REN) to confirm the entire Portuguese network was equally without voltage
- **12:36 CEST** — Red Eléctrica formally notified other European operators via the <GlossaryLink term="EAS (ENTSO-E Awareness System)">*ENTSO-E Awareness System* (EAS)</GlossaryLink>, modifying the operational status of the Iberian bloc to "Restoration"
- **12:44 CEST** — REE and the Iberian Energy Market Operator (OMIE) suspended all intraday market and balancing service activities

This suspension granted Red Eléctrica full authority to dispatch generators exclusively under technical and thermodynamic viability criteria, sidelining the customary economic *Merit Order* mechanism.

The technical recovery strategy was articulated through activation of Procedure 1.6, which mandated controlled fragmentation of the Iberian Peninsula into **seven independent operational areas**—South Zone, Tajo-Central, Levant, and others—during progressive re-energization.

![Topological fragmentation for supply restoration (P.O. 1.6)](/figuras/islas_reposicion_entsoe.png)

Each of the seven electrical islands had to <GlossaryLink term="Estabilidad de tensión">stabilize individually in terms of voltage</GlossaryLink> and frequency before synchronization with adjacent islands was authorized, thus preventing local instabilities from propagating between zones during recovery.

The emergency maneuver combined two simultaneous and complementary fronts:

- **Top-Down Strategy** — requested urgent external support from France and Morocco to provide stable external voltage and frequency reference
- **Bottom-Up Strategy** — activated autonomous startup of internal hydroelectric plants in Galicia, Asturias, and the Douro basin, the only installations capable of starting without external grid voltage

## *Black Start* Strategy

Upon confirmation of peninsular <GlossaryLink term="Colapso Q-V">voltage collapse</GlossaryLink>, REE and REN decreed immediate execution of the Bottom-Up strategy. Given that virtually all Iberian generation fleet consisted of <GlossaryLink term="IBR">*Inverter-Based Resources* (IBR)</GlossaryLink> operating in <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">*grid-following*</GlossaryLink> mode—technologically incapable of generating voltage waveforms without stable external network reference—system survival and restart fell exclusively upon **conventional synchronous machines** equipped with autonomous startup capability (<GlossaryLink term="Black Start">*Black Start*</GlossaryLink>).

![Dual Top-Down and Bottom-Up re-energization strategy](/figuras/estrategia_reenergizacion_dual.png)

The first link in this rescue chain were **hydroelectric plants**, both run-of-river and pumped storage. By not depending on auxiliary thermal systems for startup, these installations were first dispatched to energize isolated network sections and create initial "electrical islands" around the Douro, Galicia, and Asturias basins.

However, operation of these microsystems revealed difficulty in operating with minimal inertia:

- Energization of unloaded lines triggered significant capacitive transients
- Islands formed in Cantabria and Levant did not sustain and required restart
- The unit assigned to the Madrid island failed to stabilize parameters after several consecutive attempts
- Autonomous startup in Andalusia proved unsuccessful, necessitating prioritization of external support from Morocco

![Temporal deployment and effectiveness of hydroelectric Black Start](/figuras/black_start_hidroelectrico.png)

Once hydroelectric islands minimally stabilized their voltage ($V$) and frequency ($f$) parameters, the strategy shifted toward electromechanical consolidation through integration of large thermal masses. Immediate prioritization was coupling **Combined Cycle Gas (CCGT)** plants and progressively supplying auxiliary services for nuclear facilities.

Connection of these units did not aim to inject commercial energy volumes, but to act as **dynamic anchors**: they provided system <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia ($H$)</GlossaryLink> and <GlossaryLink term="Potencia de cortocircuito">short-circuit power ($S_{sc}$)</GlossaryLink> indispensable for endowing islands with necessary robustness before authorizing massive demand reconnection.

In parallel, the Portuguese electrical system executed analogous resilience scheme. REN relied upon the HPP 1-Central hydroelectric plant and CCGT 1-North combined cycle unit. At **20:22 CEST**, synchronization of Portuguese islands with Spanish zones already coupled to Continental European frequency marked the milestone guaranteeing feasibility of total Iberian Peninsula restoration.

## International Coordination

The abrupt separation of the Iberian Peninsula from the Continental European synchronous system required immediate activation of cross-border support protocols. *Regional Coordination Centers* (RCC) played a dual role: demonstrating preventive analytical limitations while serving as the organizational pillar for recovery orchestration.

In hours preceding the incident, the Coreso Regional Coordination Center had routinely executed normative operational planning tasks. All indicators and network models showed safe status ("OK"), anticipating neither congestions nor violations of the <GlossaryLink term="Criterio N-1">*N-1 Criterion*</GlossaryLink>.

![Static security validations prior to collapse](/figuras/validaciones_rcc_coreso.png)

This assessment establishes a structural limitation of the European monitoring system: RCC tools evaluate security through steady-state load flows, lacking capacity to anticipate <GlossaryLink term="Estabilidad de tensión">voltage instability</GlossaryLink> phenomena or ultrafast dynamics associated with low inertia.

Once voltage collapse occurred, European command hierarchy activated via **Synchronous Area Monitors (SAM)**, jointly operated by Swissgrid and Amprion. Between 12:49 and 12:54 CEST, these centers confirmed Emergency situation and established unified command structure:

- **REE** — frequency leader for the disconnected Iberian island
- **Swissgrid** — leadership to maintain rest of continent stability
- **RTE** — general resynchronization leader (*Resynchronisation Leader*)

Across the Pyrenean border, RTE activated bids in its internal balancing mechanism for up to 4,500 MW to sustain exports toward Spain, enabling progressive energization of northern and eastern peninsular 400 kV corridors.

![Evolution of cross-border support from France during restoration](/figuras/evolucion_carga_repuesta_francia.png)

Simultaneously, on the southern border, the Morocco interconnection operated by ONEE became the **electromechanical anchor of Andalusia**. At 13:04 CEST, support flow was enabled via the Puerto de la Cruz–Mellousa line, injecting approximately 900 MW and providing voltage reference necessary for energizing the peninsular south.

![Power injection for Top-Down support from southern border](/figuras/intercambio_marruecos_topdown.png)

## Generation Mix Evolution During Restoration

Success in progressive resynchronization required the System Operator to apply strict technological discrimination in generation dispatch. Despite solar and wind parks remaining physically intact, their reconnection was restricted during initial critical recovery phases.

This restriction stemmed from fundamental electromechanical limitation: inverters operating in <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">*grid-following*</GlossaryLink> mode **lack capacity to impose voltage waveforms**. Their phase-locking loops (<GlossaryLink term="Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)">*PLL*</GlossaryLink>) require reading a previously robust and stabilized external network to inject current. Consequently, complete re-energization responsibility fell upon sequential coupling of synchronous plants.

![Evolution of technological mix during peninsular re-energization](/figuras/evolucion_mix_reenergizacion.png)

The recovery sequence proceeded as follows:

| Time (CEST) | Milestone |
|-------------|-----------|
| 13:07 | First 31 MW supplied via Irún |
| 23:32 | 21 thermal units synchronized — 13,039 MW (≈55% of load) |
| 00:06 (Apr 29) | Reactivation of aFRR reserve master controller |
| 01:38 | First active wind park injection setpoints |
| 07:05 | Complete integration of RCR regime |
| **07:05** | **99.95% of national electricity supply restored** |

![Collapse and recovery of peninsular electrical demand](/figuras/recuperacion_demanda_peninsular.png)

Restoration of the 25 GW lost was completed after nearly **19 hours of uninterrupted maneuvers**. This episode left an operational lesson of first magnitude: in a system of high renewable penetration, recovery from total collapse critically depends on availability of conventional synchronous generation with <GlossaryLink term="Black Start">*Black Start*</GlossaryLink> capability. <GlossaryLink term="IBR">IBR</GlossaryLink> technologies, by design, cannot initiate network restoration without external voltage reference.
