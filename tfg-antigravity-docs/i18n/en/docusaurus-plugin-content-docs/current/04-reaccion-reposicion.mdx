<GlitchTitle>Response & Restoration</GlitchTitle>

Following the voltage zero at 12:33:30 CEST, the peninsular power system transitioned from an uncontrollable dynamic crisis to a phase of emergency management and structural restoration. The loss of over 15 GW and total disconnection from the continental synchronous system forced REE to abandon steady-state operating logic and activate the emergency protocols of **Operating Procedure P.O. 1.6**. Full supply restoration required nearly **19 hours** of uninterrupted manoeuvring.

## Emergency management: P.O. 1.1 and P.O. 1.6 activation

P.O. 1.1 establishes steady-state operating criteria, grounded in <GlossaryLink term="Criterio N-1">*N*−1 Criterion</GlossaryLink> compliance and the maintenance of transmission voltages between 380 kV and 435 kV. The 28 April cascade exposed the limitations of static security validation against ultra-fast dynamic transients in low- <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">inertia</GlossaryLink> networks: security limits were breached within an eleven-second window, and automatic defence schemes — including <GlossaryLink term="UFLS (Underfrequency Load Shedding)">UFLS</GlossaryLink> — proved counterproductive. REE assumed manual control under Blackout state (criterion OB3).

The institutional response was immediate and simultaneous across several fronts:

- **12:34 CEST** — REE confirms with REN that the entire Portuguese network is likewise de-energised.
- **12:36 CEST** — Formal notification to all European TSOs via the <GlossaryLink term="EAS (ENTSO-E Awareness System)">EAS</GlossaryLink>,
  updating the Iberian bloc's operational status to «Restoration».
- **12:44 CEST** — REE and OMIE suspend all intraday markets and balancing services, granting full authority to dispatch generators exclusively on technical and thermodynamic viability criteria.

The recovery strategy prescribed the controlled fragmentation of the Iberian Peninsula into **seven independent operating areas** — Southern Zone, Tajo-Centre, Levante and others — during progressive re-energisation.

![Topological fragmentation of the peninsula into seven electrical islands during restoration](/figuras/islas_reposicion_entsoe.png) _Figure 20. Topological fragmentation under P.O. 1.6. Each island had to individually stabilise its voltage and frequency parameters before synchronisation with adjacent islands was authorised. Source: ENTSO-E / REE._

The manoeuvre combined two simultaneous fronts: a Top-Down strategy (external support from France and Morocco as stable voltage reference) and a Bottom-Up strategy (autonomous start-up of internal hydroelectric plants in Galicia, Asturias and the Duero basin).

## Black Start strategy: hydroelectric plants and combined cycles

With virtually the entire generation fleet composed of <GlossaryLink term="IBR">IBR</GlossaryLink> operating in <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">grid-following</GlossaryLink> mode — technologically incapable of generating a voltage waveform without a stable external grid reference — the survival and restart of the system fell exclusively on synchronous machines equipped with <GlossaryLink term="Black Start">autonomous start-up</GlossaryLink> capability.

![Dual re-energisation strategy: Top-Down and Bottom-Up](/figuras/estrategia_reenergizacion_dual.png) _Figure 21. Dual re-energisation strategy: Top-Down via France and Morocco; Bottom-Up via internal hydroelectric plants. Source: ENTSO-E / REE._

Hydroelectric plants — run-of-river and pumped storage — were the first units dispatched to energise isolated network sections and form the initial electrical islands. However, operating these micro-systems revealed the difficulty of functioning with minimal inertia: no-load line energisation triggered significant capacitive transients that prevented the completion of multiple start-up attempts.

![Temporal deployment and effectiveness of hydroelectric Black Start attempts](/figuras/black_start_hidroelectrico.png) _Figure 22. Hydroelectric Black Start attempts during Phase 4. Grey dots represent failed attempts. The high proportion of failures reflects the complexity of energising a network without coupled synchronous mass. Source: ENTSO-E / REE._

The failures were significant: the Cantabria and Levante islands collapsed and had to be restarted; the unit assigned to Madrid failed to stabilise its parameters after several consecutive attempts; and autonomous start-up in Andalusia proved unsuccessful, requiring external support from Morocco as the voltage reference for the southern zone.

Once the hydroelectric islands achieved minimum parameter stabilisation, the strategy shifted towards electromechanical consolidation through Gas Combined-Cycle Turbines (CCGT) and nuclear auxiliary services. These units were not connected to supply commercial demand, but to form the system's **«electromagnetic scaffolding»**: they provided the short-circuit power ($S_{sc}$), inertia ($H$) and dynamic <GlossaryLink term="Potencia reactiva">reactive power</GlossaryLink> management capacity indispensable before authorising mass load reconnection.

The Portuguese system executed an analogous scheme based on HPP 1-Centro and CCGT 1-Norte. At **20:22 CEST**, the synchronisation of Portuguese islands with Spanish zones already coupled to the continental European frequency — verified via synchro-check relays — guaranteed the viability of full Iberian Peninsula restoration.

## International coordination: RCC, Swissgrid and continental resynchronisation

The tools of the Regional Coordination Centres (RCC) — in particular Coreso, responsible for Southwest Europe — evaluate security through static power flow calculations in steady-state conditions, without capability to anticipate <GlossaryLink term="Estabilidad de tensión">voltage instability</GlossaryLink> phenomena or ultra-fast dynamics associated with low inertia. Consequently, all pre-collapse analyses returned «OK» status, and the Iberian bloc remained labelled «Normal» in the EAS until the instant of the blackout.

Once the voltage zero was confirmed, the European command hierarchy activated through the Synchronous Area Monitors (SAM), operated by Swissgrid and Amprion. Between **12:49 and 12:54 CEST**, a unified command structure was established:

- **REE** → frequency leader for the disconnected Iberian island
- *Swissgrid* → stabilisation of the remainder of the continent
- **RTE (France)** → overall resynchronisation leader

Under this framework, RTE activated bids in its internal balancing mechanism for up to **4,500 MW** to sustain exports to Spain, enabling the energisation of the 400 kV corridors in the north and east of the peninsula.

![Evolution of cross-border support from France during restoration](/figuras/evolucion_carga_repuesta_francia.png) _Figure 23. Cross-border support from France during restoration. RTE injections sustained voltage stability during the re-energisation of northern and eastern corridors before sufficient internal synchronous units were coupled. Source: Government Analysis Committee._

On the southern border, the Morocco interconnection (ONEE) became the **electromechanical anchor for Andalusia**. At **13:04 CEST**, power flow was enabled via the Puerto de la Cruz–Mellousa line, injecting approximately *900 MW* and providing the voltage reference needed to energise the southern peninsula, where internal Black Start attempts had failed.

![Power injection from Morocco for Top-Down support in Andalusia](/figuras/intercambio_marruecos_topdown.png) _Figure 24. Top-Down support from the southern border (ONEE). The Moroccan interconnection proved decisive in providing the short-circuit power needed to energise Andalusia. Source: ENTSO-E / REE._

ENTSO-E's expert panel subsequently recognised this cooperation as a case of **effective coordination among European operators** under maximum severity events (OB3).

## Mix evolution and final stabilisation

Despite solar and wind farms being physically intact with primary resource available, their reconnection was restricted during the critical early phases. Grid-following inverters require a pre-existing robust external network to inject current through their <GlossaryLink term="Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)">PLL</GlossaryLink> loops: without that reference, they are inoperable. The full burden of re-energisation fell on the sequential coupling of CCGTs, remaining coal plants and nuclear auxiliary services.

![Evolution of the technology mix during peninsular re-energisation](/figuras/evolucion_mix_reenergizacion.png) _Figure 25. Technology mix during re-energisation. In the first critical hours, the system was sustained exclusively by cross-border imports and synchronous generation. IBR reconnection was restricted until minimum short-circuit power and inertia levels were accredited. Source: ENTSO-E / REE._

Load was reconnected in discrete, carefully calculated blocks:

- **13:07 CEST** — First **31 MW** supplied through Irún substation.
- **23:32 CEST** — With 21 thermal units synchronised, covered demand reached **13,039 MW** (~55% of expected load).
- **00:06 CEST (29 April)** — REE reactivated the automatic frequency restoration reserve (aFRR) master controller, restoring automated secondary regulation and load-frequency control.
- **01:38 CEST (29 April)** — First dispatch signals authorising active injection from wind farms and cogeneration plants.
- **07:05 CEST (29 April)** — Restrictions lifted for all RCR installations. REE certifies restoration of **99.95%** of national electricity supply.

![Demand collapse and recovery over the 19 hours following voltage zero](/figuras/recuperacion_demanda_peninsular.png) _Figure 26. Peninsular demand collapse and recovery. Restoration of the lost 25 GW was completed after nearly 19 hours of uninterrupted manoeuvring, with staged load reconnection to prevent further underfrequency events. Source: Government Analysis Committee._

The restoration sequence provides empirical confirmation of the central technical argument of this work: synchronous generation — absent in sufficient quantities during pre-collapse operation — proved to be the only electromechanical substrate capable of sustaining recovery. Its role as «scaffolding» for re-energisation is not an operational artefact, but a direct consequence of the fundamental physical properties that IBR in grid-following mode cannot replicate.
