<GlitchTitle>Social Impact and Emergency Response</GlitchTitle>

:::note Scope of this chapter
This chapter analyzes the first 18 hours after the electrical zero
(12:33 CEST on April 28, 2025). The technical analysis of the collapse is
developed in Chapter 3; the economic effects in Chapter 7b.
:::

The electrical collapse of 28-A was not solely a technical infrastructure failure:
it was the empirical demonstration that modern societies are
highly interdependent systems where energy, telecommunications,
water, transportation, and healthcare form a network of networks. When the first fell,
the others followed in cascade.

## 1. Emergency system response

### Spain: declaration of a national emergency

The magnitude of the power outage forced a response at the highest
institutional level. The Ministry of the Interior activated the
declaration of a national emergency under Article 28 of
Law 17/2015 of the National Civil Protection System through
Order INT/399/2025, subsequently expanded (Order INT/400/2025) to
include Castilla-La Mancha, Galicia, and the Valencian Community.

The 061 health information lines collapsed due to saturation,
forcing all critical traffic to be redirected to 112. The authorities
issued continuous messages via analog radio asking citizens
not to call 112 for information about the blackout,
reserving the channel for life-threatening emergencies.

The National Authority for Emergency and Civil Protection (ANEPC) activated the
National Operational Coordination Center (CCON) immediately.

The blackout revealed critical vulnerabilities in the SIRESP
(Integrated System of Emergency and Security Networks of Portugal) radio communication system,
which motivated the immediate approval of a technical-strategic
modernization study on May 1, 2025.

## 2. Impact on hospitals and health services

### Energy triage

In most hospitals, generator sets started within the
first 15 minutes, preserving ICUs, active operating rooms, and life support.
However, given the uncertainty about the duration of the outage, hospital
managements implemented energy triage policies: suspension of
elective interventions, outpatient consultations, and auxiliary services to
extend fuel reserves.

<ForensicReveal
  l1="Several Portuguese hospitals were about to run out of diesel
      two hours after the blackout."
  l2="Hospital diesel generators are sized for localized outages
      of a few hours, not for regional blackouts that simultaneously collapse
      supply chains. Resupplying multiple
      hospitals at once, with gas stations without electricity to
      extract fuel and streets gridlocked by traffic, was logistically
      nearly impossible."
  l3="The national commander of ANEPC, Mário Silvestre, declared before the
      parliamentary committee that the most alarming finding was that,
      barely two hours after the start of the blackout, there were hospitals with
      completely or practically depleted fuel reserves. The logistics of
      simultaneous resupply under conditions of road collapse constituted
      a national security challenge."
  source="ANEPC / Parliamentary investigation committee (Portugal, 2025)"
/>

### Victims and hoaxes

No deaths were recorded within protected clinical settings.
Massive hoaxes circulated about fatalities in hospitals in Toledo, Albacete,
Valencia, and Ourense — categorically denied by the
directors of the centers. The official toll was 8 deaths
indirectly related to the blackout:

- Carbon monoxide poisoning: use of domestic generators
  in enclosed spaces without ventilation.
- Fires: falling emergency lighting candles.
- Failures in home medical equipment: devices without the
  rigorous maintenance of the hospital environment.

:::danger Verified death — Portugal
A 77-year-old woman, on 24-hour home mechanical ventilation,
died when the respirator's battery bank ran out. The collapse of telephone
networks prevented INEM (National Institute of Medical Emergency) from
receiving the distress call in time.
:::

## 3. Paralysis of transport infrastructure

### Railway network: 35,000 people trapped

At 13:13 CEST, ADIF and RENFE confirmed the interruption of all
peninsular rail traffic. Approximately 35,000 passengers were left
trapped in trains stopped on open tracks and in metro tunnels
(Madrid, Barcelona, Lisbon).

<ForensicReveal
  l1="Tens of thousands of people were trapped inside
      trains and metros for hours."
  l2="Electric trains cannot move without the catenary. Underground
      metros were left without lighting, air conditioning, or public address systems.
      Evacuations on foot along the railway ballast required police
      coordination to avoid accidents."
  l3="Portugal required an extraordinary deployment of police forces
      to evacuate the Fertagus network. ADIF and RENFE mobilized emergency
      buses and published extraordinary guidelines for ticket
      refunds and baggage recovery. Post-28-A ministerial reports
      in Portugal propose installing large-format batteries in railway
      vehicles to give them minimum autonomy to reach the nearest station."
  source="ADIF / RENFE / Ministry of Transport (2025) / ANEPC"
/>

### Aviation: Lisbon, Porto, and Faro paralyzed

Control towers (ENAIRE/NAV) maintained operability thanks to their
redundant systems. The land side collapsed.

### Road network collapse

The simultaneous deactivation of tens of thousands of traffic lights caused
monumental gridlock. The A-6 in Madrid was completely blocked. The mayor
confirmed by radio that it was _«extremely difficult to circulate on any main axis»_,
recommending the cessation of non-essential travel.

### The gradual fall: 3 hours, 90% loss

Mobile base stations have backup batteries lasting a maximum of 2–3 hours.
As they discharged without the possibility of recharging:

### The resurrection of analog radio

In the most digitalized society in history, FM was the only informational
lifeline between authorities and citizens. Radio Nacional de España
(RNE) broadcast continuous bulletins from infrastructures with their own
diesel generators, preventing the social panic that often accompanies prolonged
information vacuums.

## 5. The water-energy nexus

### WTP, WWTP, and pumping stations

Minutes after the electrical zero, large environmental plants lost
traction. Water Treatment Plants (WTP),
Wastewater Treatment Plants (WWTP), and high-capacity pumping stations
were paralyzed.

<ForensicReveal
  l1="In several areas, the tap stopped working a few hours after the blackout."
  l2="Modern urban water does not work by pure gravity: it requires electric
      pumps to overcome topography and maintain pressure. Without electricity,
      network pressure dropped within minutes. In elevated neighborhoods, the
      shortage was immediate."
  l3="In the Region of Murcia and Toledo, a 70% reduction
      in available flows was monitored. The Horta-Guinardó neighborhood (Barcelona)
      suffered drinking water cuts for more than 18 hours. Águas de Portugal issued
      alerts for «extreme moderation» in consumption. The stoppage of WWTPs put
      receiving basins under direct risk of raw sewage spills if the
      event had exceeded 24 hours."
  source="Águas de Portugal / Barcelona City Council / ANEPC Analysis"
/>

### Post-28A response

The blackout accelerated two public policy initiatives:

1. PERTE for Digitalization of the Urban Water Cycle (>€550M): network
   sectorization, smart sensors, predictive leak control.
2. Energy self-consumption in treatment plants: promotion of anaerobic
   sludge digestion for biogas. The water sector in Spain already produced ≈787 GWh
   annually of bioenergy — goal: self-sufficiency and grid disconnection.

## 6. Asymmetric impact on vulnerable populations

### Electrodependent individuals: the invisible gap

The most critically affected segment were people whose physiology
is tied to alternating current: continuous mechanical ventilators,
oxygen concentrators, infusion pumps, home hemodialysis.

<ForensicReveal
  l1="People with home medical equipment were completely
      abandoned during the blackout."
  l2="Unlike hospitals, private residences do not have
      generator sets. Home equipment runs on the electrical grid
      or internal batteries with a few hours of autonomy. There is no proactive
      official census of electrodependent individuals that would allow prioritizing
      the delivery of portable generators."
  l3="The ALCER Federation documented that home hemodialysis patients
      could not contact their nephrologists because telephone networks had
      fallen. Treatments were rescheduled to the early morning as
      electricity was restored, avoiding direct fatalities. One documented
      case: a family of a child with cerebral palsy connected the
      oxygen concentrator to the family van battery to
      keep him alive."
  source="ALCER Federation / ANEPC / Post-28-A care documentation"
/>

### Deaf people: total isolation

The State Confederation of Deaf People (CNSE) formally denounced that
emergency information systems were exclusively focused on
oral messages via analog radio broadcasting. With the fall of digital terrestrial television (DTT) and
Internet access, the deaf community was left in a state of total informational
isolation, without guidelines on causes, dangers, or forecasts.

### Elevator entrapments

The Portuguese ANEPC documented more than 400 simultaneous incidents of people
trapped in elevators across the national territory, draining resources from
fire departments that were needed for other major contingencies.

## Conclusion: interdependence as systemic vulnerability

The sectoral analysis of the first 18 hours of the blackout allows for a concrete regulatory conclusion: the resilience of critical infrastructures cannot be planned sectorally. Reforming the water cycle, modernizing SIRESP, or reviewing hospital protocols are necessary but not sufficient conditions if they are not coordinated under a cross-cutting resilience framework that treats energy interdependence as a design variable, not an externality. 28-A demonstrated that the cost of that omission can be measured in lives, not just in megawatts.
