This chapter closes the forensic analysis by articulating two complementary planes: a comparative synthesis of institutional positions and the structural implications that the episode projects onto supply security in the context of the European energy transition. Both share a methodological premise sustained throughout the work: 28 April 2025 is not a frequency incident, but a **voltage collapse in the Q-V plane**, and that distinction is the axis on which the entire subsequent argumentative architecture pivots.

:::note Robust technical consensus across all four agents Three convergence points emerge from the contrast between the four narratives that **no expert report contradicts**:

**First**, the dominant collapse mechanism was not a frequency loss from inertia deficit, but a <GlossaryLink term="Estabilidad de tensión">voltage instability</GlossaryLink> in capacitive regime precipitated by the combination of minimum residual synchronous generation and underloaded 400 kV lines during the demand valley.

**Second**, prolonged operation under «light grid» conditions, with 82% instantaneous non-synchronous penetration, had structurally exhausted dynamic voltage control margins in the preceding weeks, as evidenced by the precursor events of 22 and 24 April.

**Third**, the regulatory framework in force on 28 April — P.O. 7.4 with asymmetric dead band, NC RfG without _Grid-Forming_ requirements — was structurally inadequate for mobilising damping resources within the timescale the transient required.

The divergences organise the institutional disagreement across two clear axes.

**The measurement perimeter axis.** The CSN Committee and REE compute inertia as a global peninsular metric (2.3 s), while IIT-ICAI and NREL reports work with disaggregated zonal values (1.3 s in the southern area). Both values are arithmetically correct and substantively different: 28 April was not a failure of the average, but of the weakest zone under local stress.

**The trigger attribution axis.** The CSN Committee and REE focus on insufficient reactive power absorption by the generation fleet — an omission by the party obligated under P.O. 7.4. IIT-ICAI focuses on the System Operator's meshing manoeuvre and the structural observability limitation on the 220 kV network through Tap-Lag — an operational decision and an instrumental limitation. Both diagnoses are compatible with the oscillographic evidence: the capacitive transient exceeding 2.4 GVAr simultaneously required a decision that triggered it and an inability to respond that prevented its containment. The disagreement is **not technical but distributive**: it concerns the attribution of responsibility within a causal chain that all reports recognise.

Read together, the four narratives do not constitute mutually exclusive interpretations of the same fact, but **partial projections of a multi-layer phenomenon** onto each agent's respective analytical framework and institutional responsibility perimeter. Interpretive divergence is not noise to be resolved in favour of one position: it is forensic data in itself, revealing how the dynamic complexity of modern power systems exceeds any individual agent's capacity to produce an exhaustive reconstruction of the incident.

## Structural implications: the energy transition trilemma

The 28 April collapse cannot be read as a random accident, but as the empirical manifestation of a set of structural tensions that the European energy transition leaves unresolved. Decarbonisation is not the cause of the collapse: it is the boundary condition that, without being accompanied by a symmetric adaptation in the technical, regulatory and economic planes, converted latent vulnerability into incident.

The Iberian power system operates under a **structural trilemma** in which each edge represents a concrete operational tension between two vertices:

| Tension | Conflicting vertices | Concrete 28 April manifestation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Synchronous displacement / low network strength** | Decarbonisation ↔ Dynamic stability | Merit order displaced CCGTs, draining the system of inertia and short-circuit power at the critical moment. |
| **Merit order / zero or negative price hours** | Decarbonisation ↔ Affordability | Over 500 zero or negative price hours in 2024; daily average price of €18.50/MWh on 28 April itself. |
| **Cost of Essential Reliability Services (ERS)** | Dynamic stability ↔ Affordability | Ancillary services for inertia, dynamic reactive power and FFR were not explicitly remunerated in the market design. |

_28 April 2025 represents the episode in which the three tensions converged without the prevailing regulatory and market framework being able to arbitrate between them._

### Technological tension

The gap between the actual generation fleet and the fleet the system needs to operate safely under IBR-dominated conditions. The replacement of large synchronous alternators by <GlossaryLink term="GFL (Grid-Following)">grid-following</GlossaryLink> inverters has deprived the network of two services that rotating machines provided without explicit remuneration: genuine <GlossaryLink term="Inercia (H)">rotational inertia</GlossaryLink> and the short-circuit power needed to sustain the SCR at transmission network nodes. The technical exit architecture — convergent across expert reports — combines distributed deployment of BESS with Grid-Forming capability for sub-cyclic voltage management with selective installation of synchronous condensers, including the <GlossaryLink term="Estrategia Brownfield">Brownfield</GlossaryLink> reconversion of decommissioned fossil plant alternators.

### Regulatory tension

The inadequacy of the inherited operating framework for governing a fast- dynamic network. The P.O. 7.4 update via the CNMC Resolution of 12 June 2025 — with explicit dynamic requirements and the first-ever inclusion of IBR and storage in the voltage control service — constitutes a step in the correct direction. However, the October 2025 rejection of raising the compliance threshold from 75% to 90% evidences the temporal asymmetry between new asset capabilities and those of the synchronous fleet they replace. At the European level, consolidation of Grid-Forming requirements in ENTSO-E's Phase II Technical Report (November 2025) and its projection onto the future NC RfG 2.0 configure the technical framework of the post-carbon grid — though the current deprioritisation of the European Commission adoption schedule creates an uncertainty window that national operators are managing through transitional measures outside the network code.

### Economic tension

The tension that expert reports identify as the ultimate constraint on the viability of the technical redesign. The pure marginalist energy model is structurally incapable of remunerating the systemic attributes that underpin stability: inertia, short-circuit power, dynamic reactive power and <GlossaryLink term="Fast Frequency Response (FFR)">Fast Frequency Response</GlossaryLink>. The proliferation of zero or negative price hours — over **500 hours in 2024**, with a daily average price of **€18.50/MWh** on 28 April itself — is not a defect of renewable design, but the signal that stability attributes are absent from the market's objective function. The transformation of these services into explicit commercial products under the category of Essential Reliability Services (ERS) constitutes the economic prerequisite without which the preceding technical and regulatory framework lacks medium-term sustainability.

At the European governance scale, the episode revealed the limitations of the EAS in fulfilling its declared early warning function. The Iberian system transitioned from «Normal» to «Blackout» state without passing through any of the intermediate states prescribed by the <GlossaryLink term="SO GL (System Operation Guidelines)">System Operation Guidelines</GlossaryLink>. The implication is structural: security tools built on static load flow analyses and the *N*−1 Criterion are inadequate for networks with high power electronics penetration, whose relevant dynamics occur on sub-second timescales. The pan-European coordination architecture requires incorporating dynamic nodal strength indicators, zonal inertia indices and grid-forming capacity metrics as standardised state parameters exchangeable between Transmission System Operators.

## The lesson of 28 April

The structural lesson of 28 April, taken in its entirety, is not that the energy transition is too ambitious or that decarbonisation compromises supply security. The lesson is that the transition, to be operationally sustainable, **must occur simultaneously across the three planes the trilemma identifies**: technical, regulatory and economic. Any asymmetry between them — a transformed generation fleet with an inherited network code; an updated network code without a market to remunerate the services it requires; a redesigned market without the physical assets capable of providing them — reproduces the vulnerability conditions that 28 April materialised.

The analytical value of this work resides, ultimately, in having shown that the four institutional narratives **coexist without cancelling each other** and that none of them, taken alone, exhausts understanding of the incident. Forensic synthesis requires treating expert reports not as rival hypotheses about the same fact, but as partial projections of a complex phenomenon onto each agent's respective framework. Only triangulated reading — which preserves divergences rather than dissolving them — allows the extraction of the structural conditions that converted a routine deep-decarbonisation operating scenario into the first systemic _blackout_ of the continental synchronous area in two decades.

Those conditions, far from being extraordinary, are present in every European power system in transition: **28 April 2025 is, in this sense, less an Iberian accident than a European precedent**.
