:::note Robust technical consensus among the four agents
From the contrast between the four narratives, three points of convergence emerge that no expert report contradicts:

First, the dominant collapse mechanism was not a frequency loss due to inertia deficit, but a voltage instability in capacitive regime precipitated by the combination of minimal residual synchronous generation and underloaded 400 kV lines during the demand valley.

Second, the prolonged operation in a "light network" regime, with instantaneous non-synchronous generation penetration of 82%, had structurally exhausted the dynamic voltage control margins in the preceding weeks, as evidenced by the precursor events of April 22 and 24.

Third, the regulatory framework in force on 28-A — P.O. 7.4 with asymmetric dead band, NC RfG without Grid-Forming requirement — was structurally inadequate to mobilize damping resources within the timeframe required by the transient.
:::

The divergences organize the institutional discrepancy along two clear axes.

Measurement perimeter axis. The CSN Committee and REE compute inertia as a global magnitude of the peninsular system (2.3 s), while the IIT-ICAI and NREL reports work with disaggregated zonal values (1.3 s in the Southern area). Both values are arithmetically correct and substantively different: 28-A was not a failure of the average, but of the weakest zone under local stress.<KeyFact id="inercia-28a" mode="callback">H = 2.3 s average / 1.3 s southern zone — Chapter 2</KeyFact>

Trigger attribution axis. The CSN Committee and REE place the focus on the insufficient reactive power absorption by the generating fleet — an omission by the entity obligated under P.O. 7.4. The IIT-ICAI places it on the System Operator's meshing maneuver and the structural unobservability of the 220 kV network by the Tap-Lag — an operational decision and an instrumental limitation. Both diagnoses are compatible with the oscillographic evidence: the capacitive transient exceeding 2.4 GVAr simultaneously required a decision that provoked it and an inability to respond that prevented containing it. The discrepancy is not technical but distributive: it refers to the attribution of responsibility within a causal chain that all reports recognize.

Read together, the four narratives do not constitute mutually exclusive interpretations of the same event, but rather partial projections of a multi-layered phenomenon onto the respective analytical frameworks and perimeters of institutional responsibility. The interpretive divergence is not noise to be resolved in favor of one stance: it is a forensic datum in itself, revealing how the dynamic complexity of modern electrical systems exceeds the capacity of any individual agent to produce an exhaustive reconstruction of the incident.

## Structural implications: resolving the Energy Transition Trilemma

As introduced at the beginning of this investigation and detailed throughout each phase of the technical, regulatory, and market analysis, the collapse of 28-A cannot be read as a random accident, but as the empirical and terminal manifestation of the **Energy Transition Trilemma**. The structural tensions that the European transition had left unresolved crystallized simultaneously that morning. Decarbonization is not the cause of the collapse: it is the boundary condition that, not being accompanied by a symmetric adaptation in the technological, regulatory, and economic planes, turned latent vulnerability into an incident.

The Iberian electrical system operates under a structural trilemma in which each edge represents a specific operational tension between two of the vertices:

### Technological tension

The gap between the actual generating fleet and what the system needs to operate safely in an IBR-dominated regime. The replacement of large synchronous alternators with Grid-Following topology inverters has deprived the grid of two services that rotating machines provided without explicit remuneration: genuine rotational inertia and the short-circuit power necessary to sustain the SCR at transmission network nodes. The technical architecture for resolution — convergent among the expert reports — combines the distributed deployment of BESS with Grid-Forming capability for sub-cycle voltage management with the selective installation of synchronous condensers, including the Brownfield conversion of alternators from decommissioned fossil fuel plants.

### Regulatory tension

The inadequacy of the inherited operational framework to govern a network with accelerated dynamics. The update of P.O. 7.4 through the CNMC Resolution of June 12, 2025 — with explicit dynamic requirements and the first-time enablement of IBR and storage in the voltage control service — constitutes a step in the right direction. However, the rejection in October 2025 of raising the compliance threshold from 75% to 90% evidences the temporal asymmetry between the capabilities of new assets and those of the synchronous fleet they replace. At the European level, the consolidation of Grid-Forming requirements in the ENTSO-E Phase II Technical Report (November 2025) and its projection onto the future NC RfG 2.0 shape the technical framework of the post-carbon grid — although the current deprioritization of the adoption timeline by the European Commission introduces a window of uncertainty that national operators are covering through transitional measures outside the grid code.

### Economic tension

The one that expert reports point to as the ultimate condition for the viability of the technical redesign. The pure marginalist energy model is structurally incapable of remunerating the systemic attributes that sustain stability: inertia, short-circuit power, dynamic reactive power, and fast frequency response. The proliferation of zero or negative price hours — more than 500 hours in 2024, with an average daily price of €18.50/MWh on 28-A itself — is not a defect of renewable design, but the signal that stability attributes are absent from the market's objective function. The transformation of these services into explicit commercial products, under the ERS category, constitutes the economic requirement without which the preceding technical and regulatory framework lacks medium-term sustainability.

At the European governance scale, the episode highlighted the limitations of the EAS in fulfilling its declared early warning function. The Iberian system transitioned from the "Normal" state to the "Blackout" state without traversing the intermediate states foreseen in the System Operation Guidelines. The implication is structural: security tools built on static load flow analysis and the N−1 Criterion are inadequate for grids with high penetration of power electronics, whose relevant dynamics occur on time scales of less than one second. The pan-European coordination architecture requires incorporating dynamic indicators of nodal strength, zonal inertia indices, and grid-forming capacity metrics as state parameters that are comparable between Transmission System Operators.

## The lesson of 28-A

The structural lesson of April 28, taken as a whole, is not that the energy transition is too ambitious or that decarbonization compromises security of supply. The lesson is that the transition, to be operationally sustainable, must occur simultaneously on the three planes that the trilemma identifies: technical, regulatory, and economic. Any asymmetry between them — a transformed generating fleet with an inherited grid code; an updated grid code without a market that remunerates the services it demands; a redesigned market without the physical assets capable of providing them — reproduces the vulnerability conditions that 28-A materialized.

The analytical value of this work lies, ultimately, in having shown that the four institutional narratives coexist without canceling each other out and that none of them, individually, exhausts the understanding of the incident. The forensic synthesis requires treating the expert reports not as rival hypotheses about the same event, but as partial projections of a complex phenomenon onto the respective frameworks of each agent. Only the triangulated reading — which preserves divergences instead of dissolving them — allows extracting the structural conditions that turned a routine operational scenario of deep decarbonization into the first systemic blackout of the continental synchronous area in two decades.

These conditions, far from being extraordinary, are present in every European electrical system in transition: April 28, 2025 is, in this sense, less an Iberian accident than a European precedent.
