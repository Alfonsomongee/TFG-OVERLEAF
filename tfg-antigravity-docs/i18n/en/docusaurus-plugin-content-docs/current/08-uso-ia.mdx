The development of this Bachelor's Thesis has taken place in an atypical methodological context: the simultaneous occurrence, during the 2024–2026 biennium, of a systemic crisis in the Iberian electricity system and an accelerated transformation in natural language processing tools. The availability of Large Language Models (LLMs) capable of processing technical documents of hundreds of pages in minutes coincided with the publication, within a year, of four primary expert reports and a dozen academic analyses on the same event. This chapter transparently documents how these tools have been integrated, what specific tasks they have performed, what systematic errors have been detected and corrected, and what epistemological reflection arises from this for research practice in engineering.

## Methodological Application: Five Operational Functions

The initial body of documents includes technical files, ENTSO-E regulations, official reports exceeding 200 pages, PSS/E simulations from IIT-ICAI, CNMC minutes, NREL oscillographic records, and a large volume of specialized press archives. Linear reading without computer mediation was incompatible with the academic deadlines of the thesis. The integration of generative models has been structured around five clearly delimited operational functions, none of which involves autonomous causal inference about the system's physics:

| Function | Description |

| Chronological Reconciliation | The four reports describe the same physical events of April 28 with different levels of granularity and time offsets of up to 200 ms between versions. | Writing Support | Suggestion of lexical variants, synonymy refinement, terminology control, and reformulation of dense academic prose passages. This support has not altered the argumentative structure or content decisions, which are the sole responsibility of the author. |
| Scaffolding for Graphical Elements | Generation of drafts in TikZ and `pgfplots` from textual descriptions, subsequently reviewed, debugged, and manually validated to ensure visual consistency and fidelity to the represented data. |

The workflow is structured in three sequential stages with a correction loop:

```text
[Primary Sources] ──> [LLM Assistance] ──> [Physical Validation Filter] ──> [Thesis Body]
                             ▲                              │
                             └──────────────────────────────┘
                               (prompt reformulation upon detected hallucination)
```

The methodologically decisive element is not the LLM assistance itself, but the physical validation filter interposed between any model output and its incorporation into the text: Kirchhoff's laws, Q-V curves, the rotor swing equation, and cross-checking with the primary source. When the filter detects an inference incompatible with the system's mechanics, the prompt is reformulated with additional semantic constraints and the cycle restarts.

## Validation and Verification: Five Cases of Erroneous Inference

The use of generative models in a domain as dense in causal relationships as power system dynamics systematically reveals a behavior that electrical systems do not tolerate: inference by statistical analogy. LLMs tend to apply "typical" causal patterns — the most frequent in their training corpus literature — to cases whose physical structure is *atypical*. The April 28 event, due to its dual nature (voltage stability instead of frequency balance), is an atypical case compared to most blackouts documented in 20th-century technical literature, making the observation of points where the AI "by default" deviated from the real phenomenon particularly productive.

| Tap-Lag | OLTCs responded correctly to the transient by proportionally raising or lowering secondary voltage. | Intentional OLTC delays — designed to prevent hunting — left them out of phase with a transient lasting tens of milliseconds, multiplying voltage towards the 220 kV and 132 kV networks. | Injection of the real time constant of the electromechanical OLTC assembly and prohibition of assuming quasi-instantaneous response. |

### The Case of the UFLS Paradox

The most illustrative case of the error pattern: when asked about the role of load shedding in the incident's development, the models systematically offered an interpretation inherited from classic blackouts, where UFLS constitutes the last line of defense and usually manages to preserve portions of the network by recovering the frequency-active power balance. This inference is physically correct for the universe of events on which the model was trained, but invalid for April 28: in a collapse dominated by the Q-V plane, UFLS activation simultaneously removed the inductive reactive power sinks provided by the disconnected loads, worsening the overvoltage instead of mitigating it.

The correction required formulating the prompt with an explicit constraint on the analysis plane and manually verifying that the resulting reasoning was consistent with the oscillography published by NREL for the time window 12:33:19–12:33:27 CEST. The phase portrait simulator is documented in [Annex IX · Methodology and Models](./anexo-metodologia-modelos-datos-vivos.mdx).

### The Case of Tap-Lag

The qualitative description that models produce of the OLTC is correct in steady state, but assumes by default a proportional and instantaneous response that the real electromechanical assembly does not possess. The intentional delay introduced to avoid hunting translated on April 28 into a transformation ratio out of phase with the electrical transient, which multiplied the overvoltage towards the 220 kV and 132 kV networks — a phenomenon invisible to the operator's SCADA, anchored in the 400 kV primary measurements. The entire causal chain of Tap-Lag required describing the OLTC time constants to the model in advance and prohibiting it from assuming quasi-instantaneous response.

### The Case of the Capacitive Contribution of Meshing

The estimated injection of 1,050 MVAr capacitive had to correspond to the effect of the shunt admittance ($Y_t$) of the 400 kV lines operating in an unloaded regime (Ferranti effect). Validation consisted of reproducing the reactive balance on the line's π model, contrasting the order of magnitude with the oscillography and the diagnosis of the IIT-ICAI report.

### The Case of the 0.6 Hz Oscillation

This illustrates a specific limitation of using AI in forensic contexts with institutional divergence. Models tend to produce an integrated response — the "most likely" reading averaged across sources — when forensic rigor requires preserving the divergence. The origin of the mode is precisely the data point the thesis must present as an unresolved discrepancy, not as an editorial consensus.

### The Case of Network Topology (Hallucination due to Lack of Data)

This case highlights a fundamental vulnerability: LLMs are designed to complete patterns. Given that the exact details of inverter control loops and specific substation topologies are not public domain for critical security reasons, the model filled the information gap by generating "typical" but fictitious network architectures, incurring a structural hallucination. AI cannot deduce the reality of closed critical infrastructures from general Internet data.

The general observation that these five cases allow formulating: the utility of automated assistance is maximum in *processing* tasks — extraction, synthesis, structuring, tabulation, assisted writing — and minimum, to the point of being counterproductive, in *causal inference* tasks about physical phenomena with low representation in training corpora.

## Critical Reflection: AI in Engineering Research

The documented experience leads to a structural conclusion, neither triumphalist nor catastrophic: generative models are powerful accelerators of the researcher's cognitive processes, but they operate on a probabilistic logic that does not coincide with the deterministic logic governing the physics of material systems.

### Methodological Implication

The use of AI does not exempt the researcher from mastering the technical content of their object of study; on the contrary, it demands it with greater severity. Only a researcher with sufficient technical competence to identify a physically impossible inference can safely use a model capable of producing it with rhetorical fluency. The asymmetry between the linguistic plausibility of the output and its material correctness constitutes the main risk associated with using these tools in demanding technical domains, and can only be neutralized through a mastery of the content that the tool cannot provide.

### Ethical-Academic Implication

Automated assistance has been used in this work as a processing tool, not as a substitute for reasoning. Every content decision — the framing of the problem, the hierarchy of sources, the formulation of the causal thesis about the April 28 collapse, the comparative assessment of institutional narratives, the final conclusions — is the sole responsibility of the author. AI does not sign a thesis nor sit before a committee: the engineer signs their name to the material causality of the facts presented, and that signature is non-transferable.

### Prospective Disciplinary Implication

The electrical systems of the next decade — dominated by power electronics, with relevant dynamics on the sub-cycle scale and with voltage-reactive interactions that the conventional operator does not capture in real-time — will require decision support tools that combine the processing speed of algorithmic models with the physical robustness of simulators like PSS/E. The integration is not trivial: simulators solve equations; LLMs approximate probability distributions.

The hybrid architecture — LLM as a semantic and synthesis interface, physical simulators as a causal inference engine — is, plausibly, the direction towards which power system engineering tools will evolve. Its development will require exactly the same epistemological vigilance that has structured the workflow of this thesis: always keeping in mind what each tool does and what it is explicitly asked not to do.
